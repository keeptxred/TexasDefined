## Minimum publishing rule for the whole event family

1. Before any new event record, search **all existing permanent guide registries**, plus synced venue, Ticketmaster and TPWD sources. Compare organizer, event identity, venue and occurrence, not title alone.
2. Verify first-party name, location, current occurrence date(s), admission distinction and organizer URL. Keep an expired historical edition clearly labeled, never silently rolling to 2027.
3. Keep evergreen page identity separate from the live occurrence. Never emit an outdated `EventScheduled` or active-ticket promise; use the existing event occurrence lifecycle.
4. A guide must tell the visitor what is uniquely useful about this event: exact venue, what makes it distinct, ticket/session choices where applicable, mobility/parking/access details where supported, and a realistic city/county itinerary.
5. Use `/county/[slug]`, relevant `/events/*` collections and verified nearby guides for two or more contextual internal connections. Never invent paths or imply every amenity applies to every festival.
6. Relevant existing travel/stay affiliate components may be used where their existing qualification and disclosure rules permit. **Do not fabricate Viator, Ticketmaster, lodging deals or commission URLs.** Official event ticketing remains the fallback.
7. Use event-specific legitimate images with verifiable usage rights; no arbitrary hero or images of unrelated venues. The site's existing image and validation rules remain authoritative.
8. Fail closed for generic, canceled and geographically misattributed names. This disposition is an editorial intake decision, not a license to promote all 60 input rows as 60 unique festivals.
