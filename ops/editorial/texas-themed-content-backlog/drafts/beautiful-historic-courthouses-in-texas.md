# 10 Beautiful Historic Courthouses in Texas Worth Building a Road Trip Around

Texas county courthouses are more than government buildings. In many towns they anchor the square, define the skyline and preserve the architectural ambition of a period when a new courthouse announced that a county expected to last.

The Texas Historical Commission says the state has more historic courthouses than any other state, with more than 240 courthouse buildings at least 50 years old. The statewide preservation program created in 1999 has helped reverse decades of demolition, fire damage and deferred maintenance.

A courthouse road trip is therefore one of the easiest ways to connect Texas architecture, county history and small-town travel.

## 1. Ellis County Courthouse — Waxahachie

The Ellis County Courthouse is one of the great showpieces of a North Texas courthouse square. Its red stone, elaborate Romanesque massing and carved detail make it the kind of building that changes the scale of downtown around it.

Waxahachie's broader stock of historic buildings makes this an easy architectural day trip from Dallas-Fort Worth.

## 2. Caldwell County Courthouse — Lockhart

Lockhart may be famous for barbecue, but its courthouse square deserves equal attention. The Caldwell County Courthouse brings color, towers and late-19th-century ornament into the center of town.

This is a perfect example of TexasDefined cross-linking in practice: architecture, county history and barbecue can all live in one itinerary.

## 3. DeWitt County Courthouse — Cuero

Cuero's courthouse gives a South Texas road trip a strong historical anchor. The building's towers and detailed exterior reward a slow walk around the square rather than a drive-by photo.

The Texas Historical Commission lists DeWitt County among the courthouses restored through the state preservation program.

## 4. Colorado County Courthouse — Columbus

The Colorado County Courthouse in Columbus sits in a town with deep Republic-era and early-statehood connections. Its restoration makes it a natural stop for travelers moving between Houston, Austin and San Antonio who want more than interstate scenery.

Pair the square with local historic sites rather than treating the courthouse as an isolated object.

## 5. Denton County Courthouse-on-the-Square — Denton

The historic Denton County Courthouse is both an architectural landmark and the visual center of one of North Texas's most active courthouse squares.

It is especially useful for showing how courthouse preservation can remain part of daily downtown life rather than freezing a building as a museum piece.

## 6. Bosque County Courthouse — Meridian

Meridian's courthouse is a strong road-trip stop for travelers heading toward the Bosque River country and the western edge of the Hill Country transition.

The smaller-town setting gives visitors a clearer sense of the original civic role of the courthouse: a dominant public building placed at the center of county life.

## 7. Goliad County Courthouse — Goliad

Goliad is already one of Texas's most historically important small towns. The courthouse adds another layer to an itinerary that can include Spanish colonial, revolutionary and 19th-century history.

This is a case where the courthouse should be part of a larger history day, not the only reason to visit.

## 8. Bexar County Courthouse — San Antonio

San Antonio's Bexar County Courthouse proves that the Texas courthouse story is not limited to small towns. The Romanesque building sits within a dense urban historic landscape near other major civic and cultural sites.

The Texas Historical Commission lists its full restoration among the projects completed through the courthouse preservation program.

## 9. Dallas County's Old Red Courthouse — Dallas

Old Red is one of the state's most recognizable urban courthouse buildings. The red sandstone and prominent towers make it an immediate visual contrast with modern downtown Dallas.

It also helps tell the story of how county government buildings can outlive their original function and remain major landmarks.

## 10. Harrison County Courthouse — Marshall

The Harrison County Courthouse in Marshall is one of East Texas's grand civic landmarks. Its scale and dome make it a natural centerpiece for a courthouse-and-history route through the region.

Marshall's broader historical landscape gives visitors enough context to make the trip more than an architecture checklist.

## Why Texas has so many courthouse landmarks

Texas has 254 counties, and for generations the county courthouse was one of the most important permanent public buildings a community constructed. Courthouses housed records, trials, elections and county government, but they also became symbols of local identity.

By the late 20th century, many historic courthouses faced serious deterioration or loss. The Texas Legislature established the Texas Historic Courthouse Preservation Program in 1999. The Texas Historical Commission reports that the program has now assisted more than 100 historic courthouses and awarded more than $400 million in grants across its history.

The lesson is visible on the road: these buildings survived because preservation became an active public project, not because old masonry takes care of itself.

## How to plan a courthouse road trip

Do not assume every courthouse is open for tourism. Some remain active government buildings with security controls, court schedules and restricted areas. Treat exterior architecture and the public square as the default experience, then verify interior access locally.

Strong courthouse loops include:
- **Central Texas:** Lockhart, San Marcos-area detours, New Braunfels, San Antonio
- **North Texas:** Denton, Waxahachie, Dallas
- **Gulf / South-Central:** Columbus, Cuero, Goliad
- **East Texas:** Marshall and nearby historic county seats

The best trip combines courthouse architecture with local food, museums, historic districts and county pages so visitors understand why the building is there in the first place.
