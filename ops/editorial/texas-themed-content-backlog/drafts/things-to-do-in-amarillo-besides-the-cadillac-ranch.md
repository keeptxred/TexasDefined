# 12 Things to Do in Amarillo Besides Cadillac Ranch

Cadillac Ranch earns the photo. It should not consume the itinerary.

Amarillo sits at the intersection of Route 66 history, Panhandle ranch culture, canyon country, museums, public art and a surprisingly deep local arts scene. Visit Amarillo itself frames the city around that contrast: cowboy culture and open landscapes alongside professional arts organizations and city attractions.

If you have a day or a weekend, these are the places that turn Amarillo from a roadside stop into a destination.

## 1. Spend a real day at Palo Duro Canyon

Palo Duro Canyon State Park is the obvious first answer because it changes the scale of the trip. The canyon sits south of Amarillo near Canyon and drops visitors into layers of red and orange rock that are invisible from the surrounding plains until the land opens.

Visit Amarillo highlights more than 30 miles of hiking, biking and equestrian trails. Heat can become the controlling factor, so summer hiking belongs early in the day and current park alerts should be checked before departure.

## 2. Drive and walk Historic Route 66 on Sixth Avenue

Amarillo's Route 66 Historic District gives the Mother Road story an actual street instead of a souvenir shelf. The district along Sixth Avenue includes vintage buildings, shops, restaurants, galleries and signs tied to the old highway corridor.

In 2026, the Route 66 centennial makes this cluster especially useful for search and internal linking. The content should outlive the anniversary by emphasizing the architecture and corridor history, not only the celebration.

## 3. Visit the American Quarter Horse Hall of Fame & Museum

Quarter horses are central to the working and competitive history of the American West, and Amarillo is a fitting place for a museum devoted to the breed and the people around it.

Visit Amarillo specifically recommends the museum among the city's distinctive attractions. Even travelers who are not horse people get a clearer picture of why ranching culture matters in the Panhandle.

## 4. See the vintage RV collection at Jack Sisemore's

A museum full of travel trailers and motorhomes sounds niche until you realize it is a history of how Americans took the road with them. Visit Amarillo has highlighted the collection's vintage RVs and travel memorabilia, including examples from the mid-20th-century road-trip era.

This is a natural companion to Route 66 content because both are about mobility, tourism and the mythology of the open road.

## 5. Explore the Panhandle-Plains Historical Museum in Canyon

The Panhandle-Plains Historical Museum at West Texas A&M University is one of the strongest ways to understand the region beyond a weekend's scenery. Its collections span regional history, art, geology, paleontology, transportation and ranching.

The Texas Historical Commission has specifically pointed travelers toward the museum as part of an Amarillo-and-Canyon history trip.

## 6. Meet Tex Randall in Canyon

Tex Randall is the kind of roadside attraction that deserves context. Visit Canyon identifies the giant cowboy as a 47-foot, seven-ton concrete-and-steel figure built in 1959 to advertise a Western store along U.S. 60.

Texas Highways reported in August 2026 that another restoration is planned for 2027. That makes Tex a useful cross-link to the roadside-attractions article while also giving Amarillo visitors a reason to spend more time in nearby Canyon.

## 7. Go to the Amarillo Museum of Art

The Amarillo Museum of Art provides a counterpoint to the cowboy-and-canyon stereotype. Its exhibitions and collection make the city's cultural scene part of the itinerary rather than an afterthought.

This stop pairs well with other attractions in Amarillo's cultural district and gives travelers an indoor option when Panhandle weather is working against outdoor plans.

## 8. Take kids to the Don Harrington Discovery Center

The Don Harrington Discovery Center is built around hands-on science and includes a full-dome planetarium experience. For families, it is one of the easiest ways to balance a long road-trip day or extreme weather with something interactive indoors.

Check current showtimes rather than embedding fixed planetarium schedules in evergreen copy.

## 9. Look for Amarillo's public-art side

Cadillac Ranch is only the most famous piece. Amarillo's broader art scene includes murals, galleries, installations and a long-running tradition of unusual public objects.

This is a good place for TexasDefined to build city-level visual inventories over time instead of stuffing every mural into one statewide list.

## 10. Eat something tied to Panhandle cattle culture

The Big Texan is the theatrical version of Amarillo's beef identity, but the larger food story is more interesting than one giant steak challenge. Ranching, cattle shipping and High Plains agriculture shaped the region's economy and food culture.

A useful food section should eventually link to local restaurant guides while keeping the evergreen article focused on the regional story rather than volatile rankings.

## 11. Use Amarillo as a base for a canyon-and-plains loop

The best Amarillo weekend extends south into Canyon and Palo Duro, west and east along Route 66, and into smaller Panhandle towns when time allows.

That makes the city a natural hub for future road-trip pages rather than a one-night stop between Oklahoma and New Mexico.

## 12. Watch the Panhandle sunset somewhere with a long horizon

One of Amarillo's simplest attractions costs nothing: the scale of the sky. The flat High Plains make weather, clouds and late light feel enormous.

Choose a safe public viewpoint, state-park location or designated stop rather than pulling onto an unsafe shoulder for a photo.

## A practical Amarillo weekend

### Day 1
- Route 66 / Sixth Avenue
- American Quarter Horse Hall of Fame or Amarillo Museum of Art
- Local dinner
- Sunset

### Day 2
- Early Palo Duro Canyon
- Panhandle-Plains Historical Museum in Canyon
- Tex Randall
- Return to Amarillo for an indoor attraction or another Route 66 stop

Cadillac Ranch fits easily into either day. The mistake is treating it as the whole city.
