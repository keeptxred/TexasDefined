# Notable Public Golf Courses in Texas

Texas golf ranges from Hill Country limestone to Gulf wind, pine forest, desert and metro resort courses. A useful article should not pretend there is one objective statewide ranking; it should identify distinct public-access experiences and verify current access before promotion.

## Course types to include

### Hill Country resort/public-access golf
Courses around San Antonio, Austin and the Hill Country can combine elevation change, limestone terrain and resort travel.

### DFW municipal and destination golf
North Texas offers major public and daily-fee facilities near a large airport market.

### Houston-area public golf
Houston-area courses often combine flat terrain, water, trees and year-round play, with summer heat and storm conditions affecting the experience.

### Gulf Coast golf
Wind, salt exposure and coastal conditions can make Gulf-area golf feel different from inland Texas.

### West Texas desert golf
El Paso and Far West Texas courses provide mountain/desert backdrops unlike Central or East Texas.

### Piney Woods golf
East Texas courses can use wooded terrain and sandy soils for a different visual and playing style.

- Brackenridge Park Golf Course — San Antonio;

- Butterfield Trail Golf Club — El Paso;
- The Golf Club of Houston public-access status where applicable;
- Lajitas Golf Resort / Black Jack’s Crossing public-play availability;
- Horseshoe Bay or other resort courses only where public/resort-guest access is confirmed;
- municipal courses in Austin, San Antonio, Dallas, Fort Worth and Houston.

## Why “public” needs verification

A course may be:

- municipal;
- daily-fee;
- resort-guest accessible;
- semi-private;
- temporarily public through stay-and-play packages;
- closed for renovation;
- operating under a new name.

Those distinctions change and must be checked directly.

## What golfers actually need to compare

- green fees and booking window;
- walking policy;
- cart requirements;
- rental clubs;
- dress code;
- practice facilities;
- course difficulty;
- summer heat exposure;
- aeration/maintenance schedule;
- nearby lodging and non-golf activities.
