# Legendary Coaches Who Built Texas Sports Programs

Texas coaching history spans high school, college and professional sports. A useful list should focus on **documented program-building impact**, not just win totals or celebrity.

## 1. Darrell Royal — Texas football

Royal’s tenure at the University of Texas reshaped the Longhorn program and helped define a major era of college football in the state.

## 2. Dana X. Bible — Texas A&M and Texas

Bible’s long coaching career linked multiple Texas institutions and helped establish early college-football traditions.

## 3. R.C. Slocum — Texas A&M

Slocum became one of the defining modern coaches in Aggie football history.

## 4. Grant Teaff — Baylor

Teaff is closely associated with Baylor’s football resurgence and later national coaching leadership.

## 5. Spike Dykes — Texas Tech

Dykes became a major figure in West Texas football culture and Texas Tech history.

## 6. Jody Conradt — Texas women’s basketball

Conradt’s success helped build national visibility for women’s basketball at Texas and remains essential to any statewide coaching-history list.

## 7. Guy V. Lewis — Houston basketball

Lewis built the University of Houston into a national basketball power and coached the Phi Slama Jama era.

## 8. Eddie Sutton — Texas college basketball connections

Sutton coached in Texas during a long national career. The final article should distinguish Texas-specific program impact from achievements elsewhere.

## 9. Marsha Sharp — Texas Tech women’s basketball

Sharp led Texas Tech to national prominence, including the program’s NCAA championship era.

## 10. Augie Garrido — Texas baseball

Garrido’s Texas tenure added national championships to an already historic coaching career and strengthened the Longhorn baseball identity.

## High-school coaching deserves its own layer

Texas high-school coaching history includes hundreds of long-tenured program builders. Rather than force a few names into a statewide list dominated by college coaches, TexasDefined should eventually create a separate high-school coaching/history page sourced from UIL, halls of fame and district archives.

## How to define “built a program”

Use evidence such as:

- sustained competitive improvement;
- championships or deep postseason success;
- program infrastructure;
- recruiting/development legacy;
- influence on women’s sports or underrepresented programs;
- coaching-tree impact;
- institutional recognition.

## Source / verification notes

- University athletics archives and halls of fame should verify tenure, records and championships.
- TSHA/Handbook of Texas may support biographies where available: https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook
- NCAA records should verify national championship claims.
- Avoid ranking coaches across sports by raw wins without methodology.
- Recheck posthumous honors, facility names and institutional records before promotion.

## Image guidance

Use one rights-cleared archival coaching image or stadium/program artifact from an institutional collection. Avoid unlicensed broadcast or sideline photography.
