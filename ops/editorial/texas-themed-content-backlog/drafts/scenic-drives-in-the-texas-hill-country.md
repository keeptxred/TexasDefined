# 12 Scenic Drives in the Texas Hill Country Worth Building a Road Trip Around

The Hill Country is better experienced as a network of river roads, ranch roads, limestone ridges and small towns than as one “perfect loop.” A good route depends on season, low-water crossings, construction, wildfire/flood conditions and what you actually want to stop for.

This guide treats each road as a corridor to build around, not a promise that every mile is always scenic or open.

## 1. Ranch Road 337 — Medina to Vanderpool to Leakey

RR 337 is one of the classic western Hill Country drives, climbing and twisting through limestone hills between Medina, Vanderpool and Leakey.

Pair it with Lost Maples, the Frio River corridor and a stop in Leakey rather than racing it as a standalone road.

## 2. Ranch Road 335 — Camp Wood area

RR 335 follows the Nueces River country north of Camp Wood and cuts through rugged ranchland.

This is open-range-style driving country where livestock, wildlife and narrow shoulders matter. Drive for the landscape, not speed.

## 3. Ranch Road 336 — Leakey north toward the Edwards Plateau

RR 336 climbs from the Frio Canyon toward the plateau and is another leg of the well-known “Three Twisted Sisters” region.

Motorcycle popularity does not make it a racetrack. Blind curves, deer and changing road conditions require conservative driving.

## 4. Devil's Backbone — Ranch Road 32

The ridge route commonly called the Devil's Backbone runs through the Wimberley–Blanco–Canyon Lake area and gives drivers broad views across the Balcones Escarpment country.

It works well as part of a Wimberley/Blanco day rather than a destination by itself.

## 5. Willow City Loop — Gillespie County

Willow City Loop is famous during wildflower season because ranchland, granite outcrops and spring color create one of the Hill Country's most photographed drives.

Much of the land along the route is private. Stay on the public road, do not block gates or driveways, and never enter a field for a bluebonnet photo without permission.

## 6. Fredericksburg to Enchanted Rock

The drive north from Fredericksburg toward Enchanted Rock combines granite-country scenery, ranch roads and one of the region's most recognizable natural landmarks.

Enchanted Rock frequently requires reservations and can reach capacity, so the road trip should not promise park entry without a current check.

## 7. Fredericksburg to Comfort via US 87 and farm/ranch-road detours

This corridor links two of the Hill Country's strongest historic-town stops and can be expanded through Luckenbach, Sisterdale or other small communities depending on route.

Use exact public roads and avoid directing readers onto private ranch lanes that appear navigable on crowd-sourced maps.

## 8. Johnson City to Blanco

US 281 between Johnson City and Blanco is a practical north-south Hill Country spine with easy access to wineries, state parks, historic sites and side roads.

The value is not that US 281 is empty—it is not—but that it connects several deeper itineraries.

## 9. Marble Falls to Llano

The Highland Lakes-to-Llano corridor transitions through granite country and gives travelers easy connections to Inks Lake, Longhorn Cavern, Buchanan-area scenery and Llano's courthouse square.

Spring wildflowers can transform the route, but bloom timing varies annually.

## 10. Kerrville to Hunt and the upper Guadalupe

The drive west from Kerrville toward Hunt follows the Guadalupe River corridor into classic camp and ranch country.

Flood history matters here. Never treat a low-water crossing, river road or weather-dependent detour as automatically passable.

## 11. Utopia to Vanderpool

The Sabinal Canyon/Utopia area gives drivers a quieter western Hill Country landscape and a natural link to Lost Maples.

Fall-color season can create heavy traffic near the natural area; reserve park entry before building a trip around it.

## 12. San Marcos to Wimberley to Blanco

This route combines the Balcones Escarpment, river towns, Hill Country backroads and easy access from Austin/San Antonio.

It is one of the best introductions for visitors who want a scenic drive without committing to the much longer western loops.

## Build the drive around conditions, not a viral map

Before leaving:

- check TxDOT road conditions and construction;
- check county/local emergency notices after heavy rain;
- verify state-park reservations and closures;
- avoid low-water crossings when water is present;
- do not stop in travel lanes for wildflowers or photographs;
- respect private-property fencing and gates;
- download offline maps where cell service is unreliable.
