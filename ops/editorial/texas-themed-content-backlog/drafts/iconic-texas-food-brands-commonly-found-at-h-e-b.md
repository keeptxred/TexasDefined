# Texas Food Brands Commonly Found at H-E-B — and How to Verify They’re Actually Texas-Based

H-E-B carries many products associated with Texas, but “sold at H-E-B” is not the same as “made in Texas” or “Texas-owned.” This guide should teach readers how to verify the connection while highlighting recognizable brands that currently appear in Texas grocery culture.

## Brand examples to verify before promotion

### Whataburger retail products
Sauces and related products have appeared in Texas grocery channels. Current licensing, production and H-E-B availability require verification.

### Blue Bell
Blue Bell is headquartered in Brenham and remains one of the state’s best-known ice-cream brands. Store availability varies by product/flavor.

### Julio’s
Texas tortilla chips, salsa and seasonings are part of the regional snack-food landscape. Current ownership/manufacturing location should be checked.

### Mrs. Renfro’s
The Fort Worth-based salsa company has a long Texas connection. Current production/headquarters details require direct brand verification.

### Fischer & Wieser
Fredericksburg-based specialty foods connect Hill Country tourism and retail food.

### Stubb’s branded sauces
The brand traces to Texas barbecue history, but current ownership and manufacturing structure must be explained separately from origin story.

### Yellowbird
Austin-founded hot-sauce branding is strongly Texas-associated. Current headquarters/production claims need current company verification.

### Siete Foods
Founded in Austin by a Mexican American family, Siete became a major packaged-food brand. Ownership changed after acquisition, so the article must distinguish Texas origin from current corporate ownership.

### Kylito’s / regional salsa makers
Smaller brands can be included only after current H-E-B listing and Texas-production verification.

### Texas pecan and honey producers
H-E-B may carry regional agricultural products, but origin should be checked product-by-product.

## Four labels readers should not confuse

1. **Texas-founded** — company originated in Texas.
2. **Texas-headquartered** — current HQ is in Texas.
3. **Texas-made** — product is manufactured/processed in Texas.
4. **Texas-grown** — agricultural ingredient itself was grown in Texas.

A product may satisfy one, several or none of these.

## Why ownership matters

Brands can remain culturally Texas-linked after being purchased by larger corporations. The article should document that evolution rather than present old ownership as current fact.

## How to verify a brand

- official company about/contact pages;
- package manufacturing/distribution label;
- Texas Secretary of State or company filings when relevant;
- Texas Department of Agriculture/GO TEXAN participation where applicable;
- H-E-B’s live product listing for current store availability.

## Source / verification notes

- H-E-B live catalog should verify current retail availability.
- Brand official sites should verify headquarters, origin and ownership.
- GO TEXAN may help identify participating Texas producers but should not be treated as proof of every product’s manufacturing origin without checking program criteria/current listing.
- Acquisition/ownership facts require current company or reliable business-source verification.
