# Indigenous Nations and Peoples With Deep Roots in Texas

Texas Indigenous history is not a closed chapter and should not be written as if Native peoples vanished when Anglo settlement expanded. This guide should introduce major nations and cultural regions while centering tribal and scholarly sources.

## Nations and peoples to cover carefully

Examples include Caddo peoples in East Texas; Comanche and Kiowa communities with strong Plains histories; Apache peoples across western regions; Tonkawa communities; Karankawa-descended communities tied to the Gulf Coast; Coahuiltecan-descended communities in South Texas; and federally recognized tribes with present-day Texas connections such as the Alabama-Coushatta Tribe of Texas, Kickapoo Traditional Tribe of Texas and Ysleta del Sur Pueblo.

## Avoid a misleading “15 tribes” checklist

Political identities, confederacies, bands, language groups and archaeological cultures are not interchangeable. Some older labels were applied by outsiders and are contested or overly broad.

## What the article should explain

- Native presence before European colonization
- Trade networks and mobility
- Spanish mission and colonial pressures
- Disease, warfare, removal and reservation policies
- Persistence, sovereignty and present-day communities
- Cultural revitalization and public-history efforts

## Place-based learning

Direct readers to tribal cultural centers, Caddo Mounds State Historic Site, museums and other public sites only where interpretation is current and respectful.

## Internal-link plan

Link to Texas history, missions, regions, archaeology, state historic sites and relevant East/West/South Texas destination pages.

## Source notes

Prioritize tribal governments and cultural programs, THC, TPWD state historic sites, NPS, Smithsonian/NMAI and peer-reviewed scholarship. Recheck terminology with current tribal preference before publication.

## Image guidance

Use tribally approved or clearly rights-cleared images of public cultural sites, landscapes or historical materials. Avoid generic “Native American” stock imagery and sacred objects without context.
