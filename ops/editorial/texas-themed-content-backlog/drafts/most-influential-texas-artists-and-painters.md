# Texas Artists Who Changed How the State Was Seen

Texas visual art is larger than bluebonnets and cowboy paintings. Artists working in the state have documented open-range life, shaped public monuments, absorbed borderlands influences, built modern art institutions and pushed Texas work into national and international conversations.

This is a starting map, not an objective ranking.

## 1. Frank Reaugh

Humanities Texas describes Frank Reaugh as the “dean of Texas artists.” His paintings and pastels recorded cattle, longhorns and broad Texas plains while those landscapes were changing rapidly.

His work is especially useful for linking art to ranching and environmental history rather than treating Western imagery as generic decoration.

## 2. Elisabet Ney

Ney came to Texas after an established European sculpture career and later created the well-known Sam Houston and Stephen F. Austin statues displayed at the Texas Capitol.

Her Austin studio became a museum, giving TexasDefined a physical place to connect biography, public art and architecture.

## 3. Robert Rauschenberg

Born in Port Arthur, Rauschenberg became one of the most influential American artists of the twentieth century through his experiments with painting, found objects, printmaking, photography and performance.

Humanities Texas includes him in its Texas Originals program.

## 4. Georgia O’Keeffe — Panhandle chapter

O’Keeffe was born outside Texas, so the article should not label her a native Texan. Her years teaching in Amarillo and Canyon nevertheless helped shape an important period in her artistic development.

The Handbook of Texas notes the influence of the North Texas plains on her developing aesthetic.

## 5. Tom Lea

El Paso artist and writer Tom Lea worked across murals, illustration, war art, portraiture and literature.

His work makes El Paso a useful anchor for Texas visual culture beyond the state’s central and eastern cities.

## 6. José Cisneros

El Paso illustrator José Cisneros became known for historically researched images of Spanish colonial and borderlands subjects.

A final draft should connect his work to the visual interpretation of Texas border history while distinguishing illustration from primary historical evidence.

## 7. Porfirio Salinas

Salinas became closely identified with Hill Country landscapes and Texas wildflowers.

His place in the article should help readers understand why bluebonnet painting became a recognizable Texas visual tradition without implying he invented it.

## 8. Julian Onderdonk

San Antonio painter Julian Onderdonk helped establish an earlier Texas landscape tradition, including fields and seasonal scenes around Central and South Texas.

## 9. Allie Victoria Tennant

Sculptor Allie Tennant’s public works and Texas commissions broaden the list beyond easel painting and male-dominated narratives.

Humanities Texas includes Tennant among Texas Originals and provides a strong biographical starting point.

## 10. Modern and contemporary Texas art requires a wider lens

A final live article should expand beyond historic canon through museum collections and current institutional scholarship, including Mexican American, Black, Indigenous, women and contemporary Texas artists.

Do not convert “influential” into a numeric ranking unless a transparent methodology exists.
