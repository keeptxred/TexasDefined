# Texas Athletes Who Made Olympic History

Texas has produced, trained or become home to generations of Olympians, but those are three different claims. A careful article should say whether an athlete was born in Texas, lists a Texas hometown, attended a Texas school, trained in the state or simply became closely associated with Texas later.

This draft prioritizes athletes with clear Texas ties and major Olympic accomplishments. Medal totals and hometown fields should be refreshed from Team USA before publication.

## 1. Simone Biles — Spring

Team USA lists Simone Biles’ hometown as Spring, Texas. Across Rio 2016, Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024, she became an 11-time Olympic medalist with seven gold medals.

Her Texas connection is not incidental: Team USA notes that she attended school in Harris County before homeschooling as her gymnastics training intensified.

Biles belongs near the top of any Texas Olympic-history article because her career changed the standard for American gymnastics while remaining strongly rooted in the Houston area.

## 2. Sha’Carri Richardson — Dallas

Team USA lists Richardson’s hometown as Dallas.

At Paris 2024, she won Olympic gold in the women’s 4x100-meter relay and silver in the 100 meters. Her entry gives the page a contemporary Texas track-and-field connection without pretending her career is finished.

Future Olympic results should be appended rather than hard-coded as a permanent career total.

## 3. Brittney Griner — Houston

Team USA lists Houston as Brittney Griner’s hometown and Baylor University as her college.

She won Olympic basketball gold in 2016, 2020 and 2024. Her career links Houston upbringing, Baylor basketball and the long dominance of the U.S. women’s national team.

## 4. Gabby Thomas — Texas academic and training ties

Gabby Thomas is not a native-Texas example in the same way as Biles or Richardson, so the article should label her connection precisely.

Team USA notes that Thomas earned a master’s degree in epidemiology at the University of Texas Health Science Center at Houston. At Paris 2024 she won three gold medals, including the 200 meters and relay events.

This is a useful example of how Texas universities and training environments can become part of an Olympian’s story even when the athlete’s hometown is elsewhere.

## 5. Carl Lewis — Houston track legacy

Carl Lewis became deeply associated with Houston through the University of Houston and his training career.

His Olympic record across the 1980s and 1990s made him one of the defining American track-and-field athletes of the twentieth century.

Before publication, use Team USA/USOPC or Olympic historical records for exact medal totals and avoid compressing his long career into one event.

## 6. Mary Lou Retton — Houston-area gymnastics legacy

Retton’s 1984 all-around gold became a landmark in U.S. gymnastics history, and her training connection to the Houston area made her part of Texas sports culture.

Because biography details can be repeated inaccurately online, verify birthplace, residence and training history independently rather than labeling her “from Texas” without qualification.

## 7. Michael Johnson — Dallas roots

Sprinter Michael Johnson is one of the most recognizable Texas-linked Olympians of the modern era.

His career combined Olympic titles with a distinctive running style and record-setting performances in the 200 and 400 meters.

Use Team USA/Olympic records for exact Games-by-Games medal details before promotion.

## 8. Sanya Richards-Ross — Austin training and Texas track culture

Richards-Ross became strongly associated with Austin and the University of Texas while building an Olympic career in the 400 meters and relays.

Her entry helps connect elite international sport to Texas collegiate track programs.

## 9. Michelle Carter — North Texas throwing tradition

Shot-putter Michelle Carter became the first U.S. woman to win Olympic shot-put gold when she won at Rio 2016.

Her Texas family and school connections make her a valuable addition because Olympic history is not only sprinting, swimming and gymnastics.

## 10. Laura Wilkinson — The Woodlands

Diver Laura Wilkinson’s Olympic platform gold in 2000 is one of Texas swimming/diving culture’s memorable Olympic stories.

Her career links The Woodlands area to a sport often overshadowed by Texas football and track.

## 11. Johnny “Lam” Jones and Texas multi-sport speed

Texas Olympic history intersects with football history through athletes who competed internationally in track before or alongside professional careers.

Any inclusion here should be sourced carefully through Olympic and university archives so the article does not repeat nickname or medal-history errors.

## 12. The list should keep growing

The point of this article is not to declare an immutable “12 greatest.” Texas continues to send athletes to Summer and Winter Games, and Team USA’s athlete database changes after each Olympics.

A live page should maintain two layers:

- an evergreen historical core;
- a current callout for the newest Texas-linked medalists.

## How Texas creates Olympic pathways

Texas contributes through:

- large high-school sports systems;
- NCAA programs;
- elite gymnastics clubs;
- swimming/diving programs;
- track-and-field infrastructure;
- major medical and sports-performance centers.

That makes the article useful for cross-linking Sports, Education, major metros and Texas college coverage.
