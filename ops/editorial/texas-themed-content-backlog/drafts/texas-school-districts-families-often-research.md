# 10 Texas School Districts to Research When Comparing Major Relocation Corridors

There is no responsible statewide ranking of “best Texas school districts” that can tell a family where to move. Districts are large, individual campuses differ, boundaries can change, programs vary by grade, and a letter grade is only one part of the picture.

A better relocation guide uses representative districts as case studies and then teaches families how to investigate the exact address and campus assignment themselves.

The ten districts below span several fast-growing Texas relocation corridors. They are not ranked and their inclusion is not an endorsement.

## 1. Frisco ISD — North Texas

Frisco ISD covers parts of the northern Dallas-Fort Worth growth corridor.

What to research: rapid enrollment growth, campus assignments, attendance boundaries, high-school structure, specialty programs, commute patterns and housing costs across the district footprint.

## 2. Prosper ISD — North Texas

Prosper ISD sits in another rapidly developing North Texas corridor.

What to research: new-campus openings, boundary adjustments, construction, transportation and how fast-growing subdivisions may affect future assignments.

## 3. McKinney ISD — Collin County

McKinney ISD serves much of McKinney, but city limits and school-district boundaries are not interchangeable.

What to research: exact-address assignment, programs, campus-level outcomes, commute to employment centers and the tax/housing tradeoffs between nearby communities.

## 4. Katy ISD — west Houston area

Katy ISD spans portions of the west Houston suburban area across more than one local jurisdiction.

What to research: exact campus zoning, fast-growing western neighborhoods, transportation, new-school construction and how a “Katy” mailing address can relate to multiple governmental boundaries.

## 5. Fort Bend ISD — southwest Houston area

Fort Bend ISD serves a large and diverse suburban area southwest of Houston.

What to research: campus-by-campus differences, program availability, transportation, changing enrollment patterns and the relationship among city, county and district boundaries.

## 6. Conroe ISD — north Houston / Montgomery County

Conroe ISD serves a large portion of the north-Houston growth corridor.

What to research: distance and commute inside a geographically large area, rapid development, school construction, attendance-zone changes and local housing/property-tax context.

## 7. Round Rock ISD — Austin metro

Round Rock ISD serves parts of the north Austin metropolitan area.

What to research: exact municipal/district boundaries, campus assignments, housing cost, commute corridors, specialized programs and how growth changes the map over time.

## 8. Leander ISD — northwest Austin area

Leander ISD covers a major northwest Austin suburban corridor that has grown rapidly.

What to research: new developments, boundary changes, transportation, campus capacity and the distance between a home's mailing address and the family's actual daily school/commute pattern.

## 9. Hays CISD — south of Austin

Hays CISD serves communities along the Austin-San Antonio growth corridor, including areas around Kyle and Buda.

What to research: rapid population growth, campus openings, boundary adjustments, commute patterns and the infrastructure consequences of fast residential development.

## 10. Northside ISD — San Antonio area

Northside ISD is one of the large San Antonio-area districts and covers a broad suburban geography.

What to research: campus-level differences, program availability, transportation, housing/commute tradeoffs and the fact that a large district cannot be summarized by one rating.

## Start with TXschools.gov, but do not stop there

TXschools.gov is the Texas Education Agency's official public school-reporting site. As of August 14, 2026, the site has been updated with current public data, including 2026 accountability information.

Use it to look up both the **district and the exact campus**. Do not assume a district-wide rating describes every elementary, middle or high school.

## Understand what the 2026 A–F system measures

TEA's 2026 accountability system evaluates schools and districts through defined performance domains and rules. Those ratings are useful standardized information, but they do not measure every factor a family may care about.

A family may also need to investigate:

- course and advanced-program availability;
- special education services;
- bilingual/dual-language programs;
- career and technical education;
- fine arts and athletics;
- student support and counseling;
- transportation;
- class size and staffing;
- campus climate;
- school start times;
- extracurricular access;
- transfer policies.

## Verify the exact address before choosing a house

A real-estate listing, ZIP code or city name is not enough. Before making a housing decision:

1. Use the district's official attendance-boundary tool for the exact street address.
2. Confirm the currently assigned elementary, middle and high school.
3. Ask whether boundary changes or new campuses are already approved.
4. Check TXschools.gov for the assigned campuses, not only the district.
5. Review the district's own program and enrollment pages.
6. If a specialized program matters, confirm eligibility and whether transportation is provided.

Save screenshots or written confirmation when school assignment is a major purchase factor, and recheck close to closing because attendance zones can change.

## A–F ratings are not home-value guarantees

School ratings can influence buyer perceptions, but they should not be treated as a promise of future resale value or as a complete description of educational quality.

Ratings can change as student performance, methodology and district circumstances change. Housing markets also respond to interest rates, supply, taxes, insurance, commute and many other factors.

## Compare taxes separately from school performance

Texas school districts are taxing units as well as education systems. The tax rate, exemptions, bond obligations and other local taxing units attached to a property affect the household budget.

Do not infer the tax bill from a school rating or district reputation. Use the exact property and current appraisal/tax data.
