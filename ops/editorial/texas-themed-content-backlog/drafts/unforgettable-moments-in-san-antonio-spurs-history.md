# Moments That Define San Antonio Spurs History

The Spurs are one of Texas sports’ defining franchises because their history spans the ABA, five NBA championships, the Robinson–Duncan transition, an international core, Gregg Popovich’s long coaching era and a new generation that returned San Antonio to the NBA Finals in 2026.

A current history page must include that 2026 conference title even though it did not end with a championship.

## 1. The ABA roots

The franchise that became the Spurs arrived in San Antonio during the ABA era before joining the NBA in the 1976 merger.

That origin matters because San Antonio’s pro-basketball identity predates the championship years by decades.

## 2. George Gervin defines the early Spurs

George “The Iceman” Gervin became the first iconic Spurs superstar and one of the franchise’s foundational Hall of Famers.

His scoring years established San Antonio as more than a transplanted ABA team.

## 3. David Robinson arrives

Robinson became the face of the franchise through the 1990s and eventually a bridge between two eras of Spurs contention.

His combination of individual awards, community identity and longevity made him central to San Antonio sports culture.

## 4. The 1997 draft changes everything

The Spurs selected Tim Duncan with the first pick in the 1997 NBA Draft.

Pairing Duncan with Robinson immediately changed the franchise’s ceiling and created the foundation for the first championship.

## 5. The 1999 championship

San Antonio won its first NBA championship in 1999.

NBA records list the Spurs as champions in 1999, beginning a five-title run spread across 16 seasons rather than one short dynasty burst.

## 6. The 2003 title and Robinson’s farewell

The Spurs won again in 2003, giving David Robinson a championship in his final season.

That title also marked the transition toward a core increasingly centered on Duncan, Tony Parker and Manu Ginóbili.

## 7. The international core takes shape

Parker and Ginóbili helped make the Spurs one of the NBA’s most internationally identified championship teams.

Their careers also expanded the franchise’s connection to global basketball without changing its distinctly San Antonio identity.

## 8. The 2005 Finals

San Antonio defeated Detroit in a seven-game Finals to win its third NBA championship.

The series became a defining example of the defense, execution and resilience associated with the mid-2000s Spurs.

## 9. Tony Parker’s 2007 Finals MVP

The Spurs won their fourth championship in 2007.

Parker became NBA Finals MVP, an important milestone in both Spurs history and the rise of international stars in the league.

## 10. The 2013 heartbreak

San Antonio came painfully close to another championship before losing the 2013 NBA Finals to Miami in seven games.

The loss matters because it sets up the response that followed one year later.

## 11. The 2014 redemption championship

The Spurs returned to the Finals and defeated Miami in 2014 for the franchise’s fifth championship.

That team became associated with unselfish ball movement and one of the most admired versions of the Popovich-era system.

## 12. Tim Duncan retires as the franchise statistical anchor

NBA’s current Spurs team page lists Duncan as the franchise leader in points, rebounds and blocks.

His retirement closed the playing career of the central figure in all five Spurs championships.

## 13. Parker and Ginóbili enter the Hall of Fame

Ginóbili entered the Hall of Fame in 2022 and Parker followed in 2023, joining Duncan and Robinson among the franchise’s Hall of Fame figures.

Their inductions turned the famous Spurs core from recent memory into formally recognized basketball history.

## 14. A new generation returns San Antonio to the Finals in 2026

The NBA’s official 2026 record lists San Antonio as Western Conference champion and an NBA Finals participant for the first time since 2014.

The New York Knicks defeated the Spurs 4–1 in the 2026 Finals.

That run belongs in the franchise timeline even without a sixth title because it marks the clearest break from the long post-Duncan transition period.

## 15. The franchise enters another era

A sports-history page should not declare the 2026 team’s long-term legacy before it happens.

Instead, the article should treat the conference title as the start of a new chapter and refresh after each season to see whether it becomes a one-year breakthrough or the beginning of another sustained contender.

## The numbers that define the franchise

NBA’s current Spurs page lists:

- NBA championships: **1999, 2003, 2005, 2007, 2014**;
- Western Conference titles including **2026**;
- Duncan as all-time franchise leader in points, rebounds and blocks;
- Parker as all-time franchise assists leader;
- Ginóbili as all-time franchise steals leader.
