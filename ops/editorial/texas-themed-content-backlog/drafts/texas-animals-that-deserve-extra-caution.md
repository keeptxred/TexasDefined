# Texas Animals That Deserve Extra Caution

Texas wildlife is not a reason to stay indoors. Most encounters are uneventful when people give animals space, avoid feeding them and understand where risk is highest.

The useful question is not “What are the deadliest animals in Texas?” It is “Which animals deserve extra caution, and what should a visitor actually do?”

## 1. American alligators

Alligators occur primarily in East Texas, coastal wetlands and connected waterways. TPWD advises people to keep their distance, never feed alligators and use designated swimming areas where swimming is allowed.

## 2. Venomous snakes

Texas has several venomous snake groups, including rattlesnakes, copperheads, cottonmouths and coral snakes. Most bites occur when a snake is stepped on, handled or intentionally approached.

## 3. Feral hogs

Feral hogs can be large, fast and defensive when cornered. They also create substantial ecological and agricultural damage. Give them space and keep pets from chasing them.

## 4. Mountain lions

Mountain-lion encounters are uncommon, but West and South Texas contain suitable habitat. Follow current TPWD guidance for sightings and never approach a lion for a photograph.

## 5. Black bears

Black bears are increasingly documented in parts of West and Northeast Texas. Campsite food storage and distance matter more than panic.

## 6. Coyotes

Coyotes are widespread and frequently live near suburbs. Problems increase when people intentionally or unintentionally feed them.

## 7. Bobcats

Bobcats are usually secretive and avoid people. Treat a close or unusually bold animal cautiously, particularly if it appears sick or injured.

## 8. Javelinas

Javelinas occur in South and West Texas. They are not pigs, and they should not be fed or cornered.

## 9. Stingrays

Shallow Gulf waters can hold stingrays. Coastal safety guidance often recommends shuffling feet in suitable shallow-water habitat rather than stepping down abruptly.

## 10. Sharks

Sharks occur naturally in the Gulf. Risk to swimmers is low compared with ordinary beach hazards, but anglers and swimmers should follow local warnings and avoid handling sharks without appropriate knowledge.

## 11. Fire ants

Imported fire ants are a practical everyday hazard because their stings can trigger serious allergic reactions in some people.

## 12. Scorpions

Scorpions are common in parts of Texas and sometimes enter homes, shoes, garages and campsites. Shake out gear and avoid blind hand placement in rocky or wood-pile areas.

## 13. Africanized honey bees and defensive bee colonies

Most bees are not a problem when left alone. A disturbed defensive colony can be dangerous, especially when people cannot quickly get indoors or into a vehicle.

## 14. Ticks

Ticks deserve more attention than dramatic predators because they can transmit disease. Repellent, clothing, checks after hiking and prompt removal are practical risk controls.

## 15. Mosquitoes

Mosquitoes are another ordinary but consequential wildlife risk. Current public-health guidance should control disease-specific language because mosquito-borne disease activity changes over time.

## What not to do

- do not feed wildlife;
- do not handle snakes or injured animals;
- do not approach young wildlife assuming it is abandoned;
- do not rely on social-media species identification for medical decisions;
- do not use lethal control where law or safe professional alternatives apply without first checking current rules.
