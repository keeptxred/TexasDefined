# Famous Songs Written About Specific Texas Women

Songs associated with real women are especially prone to legend. This article should include only cases where the identity of the woman and the songwriting connection can be supported by songwriter, family, archival or reputable music-history sources.

## What counts

A song qualifies when a specific Texas woman is credibly documented as the subject, inspiration or namesake. A song that merely uses a common female name should not be forced into the list.

## Research questions for each entry

- Who wrote the song?
- Who recorded the best-known version?
- Is the woman a documented real person?
- What is her Texas connection?
- Did the songwriter identify her directly, or is the connection later folklore?
- Are competing origin stories documented?

## Avoid collapsing women into muses

Where possible, explain the woman’s own life, work and agency rather than treating her only as someone who inspired a famous man’s song.

## Copyright boundary

Do not reproduce lyrics. Summarize the song’s theme and the documented origin story, using only minimal quoted wording if identification requires it.

## Internal-link plan

Link to Texas musicians, women in Texas history, city/county pages, dance halls and country-music influence.

## Source notes

Use songwriter interviews, publishing records, artist archives, Country Music Hall of Fame resources, university collections and well-sourced biographies. Label disputed inspiration stories explicitly.

## Image guidance

Use rights-cleared portraits of documented subjects where available, or a relevant Texas music-history location. Avoid album covers without permission.
