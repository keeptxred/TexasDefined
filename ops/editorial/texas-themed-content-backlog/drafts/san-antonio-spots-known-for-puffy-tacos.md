# San Antonio Puffy Tacos: Where the Tradition Starts and How to Find It Today

The puffy taco is one of San Antonio’s most distinctive foods: fresh corn masa is fried so the shell inflates into a crisp, airy exterior with a softer interior. The useful story is not a generic “10 best tacos” ranking. It is the Lopez-family history, the two current anchor restaurants and the way the style spread through the city.

## 1. Ray’s Drive Inn — the origin story

Current Visit San Antonio material credits Ray’s Drive Inn with inventing the puffy taco in the 1950s.

Ray’s own site calls itself the “Home of the Original Puffy Taco” and lists its current address at 822 SW 19th Street.

Because origin stories can become simplified over time, the live article should attribute the claim rather than present every disputed detail as independently proven.

## 2. Henry’s Puffy Tacos — the family tradition expands

Henry’s Puffy Tacos traces its history to the same Lopez family. Its current history page says Henry Lopez opened Henry’s Puffy Tacos in 1978 after earlier family food businesses and experimentation with puffed fried masa tacos.

The business currently lists two San Antonio locations: Bandera Road and West Woodlawn Avenue.

## 3. What makes a puffy taco different

The shell starts from corn masa rather than a preformed hard taco shell. Frying causes it to expand, creating a thicker, airy shell that is crisp but not brittle in the same way as a standard hard taco.

The taco is then filled while the shell is fresh.

## 4. Traditional fillings

Common fillings include seasoned beef, chicken, carne guisada and other Tex-Mex combinations. Current menus should be checked before naming exact options at a restaurant.

Henry’s current menu confirms puffy tacos remain an active menu item, but exact fillings/prices can change.

## 5. Puffy tacos are a San Antonio identity marker

The food has become more than one restaurant dish. It appears in local sports and festival culture, including Henry the Puffy Taco mascot associated with San Antonio baseball.

That gives the article an internal-link path into Fiesta, sports and local culture rather than only restaurant search traffic.

## 7. Do not turn family history into a fake rivalry

Henry’s own history page describes the Ray’s/Henry’s relationship as a shared family legacy and acknowledges friendly debate about naming and recognition.

The article should preserve that nuance instead of manufacturing “who really invented it?” conflict for clicks.
