# 12 Things to Understand About the Texas Real Estate Market in 2026

There is no single Texas housing market. A buyer comparing Austin, Houston, San Antonio, Fort Worth, a Gulf Coast town and a West Texas oil community is looking at different inventory, insurance, taxes, construction pipelines and local economies.

The statewide numbers are still useful, but they should be treated as a dashboard—not a price guide for a particular address.

TexasDefined should refresh every market statistic in this article before publication. The current draft is anchored to the Texas Real Estate Research Center's July 2026 report, which primarily analyzes May 2026 data.

## 1. The statewide market is showing signs of stabilization, not a universal boom

The Texas Real Estate Research Center described the market in July 2026 as showing early signs of stabilization after a period of softening. Affordability, elevated mortgage rates, high inventory and economic uncertainty were still restraining activity.

That is a very different market from the pandemic-era bidding-war narrative many buyers still carry in their heads.

## 2. Inventory is much more available than it was during the shortage years

The Research Center reported roughly 150,300 active Texas listings in May 2026 and about 5.3 months of supply statewide.

That creates more choice and negotiating room in many places, but statewide supply hides huge metro differences. A buyer should check the exact city, neighborhood and property type.

## 3. Statewide prices have been relatively flat to modestly softer

The statewide median sales price was about $340,000 in May 2026, roughly unchanged from a year earlier, while the Research Center's home-price index showed a modest year-over-year decline.

Do not interpret that as “Texas homes are down X percent” everywhere. Austin, San Antonio, Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth and smaller metros are moving differently.

## 4. Austin is not the whole Texas market

Austin experienced one of the state's sharper corrections after its pandemic-era run-up. By May 2026 the Research Center reported a larger year-over-year price decline there than statewide.

That does not make Austin a proxy for Texas. Fort Worth was showing a different trajectory, while Houston and San Antonio had their own inventory and pricing pressures.

## 5. A statewide median says almost nothing about the monthly cost of one home

Purchase price is only the starting point. Texas buyers also need to model:

- mortgage rate and down payment;
- property taxes from every taxing unit attached to the address;
- homeowners insurance;
- flood and wind coverage where relevant;
- HOA dues;
- MUD/PID or other special-district costs;
- utilities and summer cooling load;
- commute/toll costs;
- maintenance appropriate to the local climate.

TexasDefined should send readers from this article into the mortgage, property-tax, homestead and insurance tools instead of pretending median price equals affordability.

## 6. Property taxes are hyperlocal

Texas has no state property tax. Local taxing units levy property taxes, and two similarly priced homes can have meaningfully different effective burdens because of school districts, cities, counties and special districts.

The correct comparison is exact-address tax history and taxing-unit structure, not a generic “Texas property tax rate.”

## 7. Insurance can change the deal after you think you know the price

Roof age, hail exposure, wildfire conditions, Gulf wind exposure, flood risk, replacement cost and carrier appetite can materially affect insurance pricing and availability.

Get property-specific quotes before assuming a home fits the budget. For coastal and flood-exposed properties, understand which risks require separate coverage.

## 8. New construction can distort first-year tax expectations

Fast-growing Texas metros contain enormous amounts of new construction. A lender's first tax estimate may be based on incomplete appraisal history, land value or a partially completed improvement.

Buyers should understand what taxes may look like once the finished property is fully appraised and all applicable district rates are reflected.

## 9. More listings can shift negotiating power without creating a bargain everywhere

Elevated inventory generally gives buyers more alternatives, more time and more leverage than a severe shortage market. The Research Center also reported meaningful seller price reductions in 2026.

But a desirable house in a tight submarket can still attract competition. “Buyer's market” is too broad to apply without neighborhood-level context.

## 10. Days on market matter, but the history matters more

The statewide average time before sale was longer in 2026 than during the hottest pandemic years. Buyers should go deeper than one number:

- How long has the current listing been active?
- Was it previously withdrawn or relisted?
- How many price cuts occurred?
- Did prior contracts fall through?
- Are nearby comparable homes actually closing or merely listed?

A stale listing can represent opportunity, condition problems, pricing problems or all three.

## 11. Texas growth does not eliminate cyclical risk

Population growth, job formation and household creation support long-term housing demand, but real estate remains sensitive to interest rates, construction supply, local employment and affordability.

Cities tied heavily to one industry can move differently from diversified metros. Builders can also add supply quickly in fast-growing suburban corridors.

“People are moving to Texas” is not, by itself, an investment thesis for every house.

## 12. The exact address matters more than the statewide forecast

Before buying, compare the property on five layers:

1. **Market:** neighborhood sales, inventory, price reductions and days on market.
2. **Taxes:** appraisal district, exemptions, taxing units and special districts.
3. **Insurance/hazards:** flood, hail, wind, wildfire, roof and replacement cost.
4. **Infrastructure:** roads, utilities, schools, drainage and planned development.
5. **House:** inspection, foundation, HVAC, roof, plumbing, energy efficiency and maintenance history.

That is a much stronger decision framework than trying to predict whether Texas home prices will be 2 percent higher or lower next year.

## Current 2026 snapshot to refresh before publication

The July 2026 Texas Housing Insight report, based largely on May data, reported:

- about 33,661 statewide closed sales in May;
- a statewide median sales price around $340,000;
- about 150,303 active listings;
- roughly 5.3 months of supply;
- statewide home-price index about 0.6 percent below a year earlier;
- very different inventory conditions across individual metros.
