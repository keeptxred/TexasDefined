# Famous Movies Filmed in Texas — and the Places You Can Still Recognize

A Texas film guide should separate **set in Texas** from **actually filmed in Texas**. Those are not interchangeable.

The Texas Film Commission maintains a filmography and official Film Trails built around productions with verified Texas filming locations. That is a stronger foundation than entertainment-site lists that infer locations from a movie’s story.

## 1. *Giant* (1956)

West Texas scenery became central to one of the state’s most recognizable film legacies. The Texas Film Commission includes *Giant* on its Texas Classics Film Trail.

## 2. *Bonnie and Clyde* (1967)

The Film Commission also includes *Bonnie and Clyde* in its classics trail, connecting the production to Texas locations and the state’s broader outlaw-history mythology.

## 3. *The Last Picture Show* (1971)

Peter Bogdanovich’s film is closely identified with Archer City and North Texas. Its small-town streetscape made place itself part of the story.

## 4. *The Texas Chain Saw Massacre* (1974)

This landmark horror film was made in Central Texas and is included in the Texas Film Commission’s classics trail.

A tourism article should verify what filming sites are legally accessible today and never encourage trespassing at private properties.

## 5. *Tender Mercies* (1983)

Texas landscapes and small communities have repeatedly served films whose visual identity depends on open space rather than metropolitan skylines.

Use the Film Commission filmography to verify exact production locations before promotion.

## 6. *Lonesome Dove* (1989) — miniseries exception

Although not a theatrical film, *Lonesome Dove* belongs in Texas screen history strongly enough to merit a labeled sidebar rather than quietly pretending it is a movie.

The Texas Film Commission includes it in the Texas Classics Film Trail.

## 7. *Dazed and Confused* (1993)

Richard Linklater’s Austin-area production helped turn ordinary Central Texas locations into cult-film landmarks.

The Film Commission’s Richard Linklater Texas Film Trail provides an official route into those locations.

## 8. *Hope Floats* (1998)

The production used Texas small-town locations that remain a draw for fans. Verify current public access to any featured building before converting a film location into a travel recommendation.

## 9. *Office Space* (1999)

Austin-area offices, roads and restaurants helped create the anonymous suburban-office world of *Office Space*.

Its Texas production history is part of the Film Commission’s classics trail.

## 10. *The Newton Boys* (1998)

Another Linklater production, *The Newton Boys* connects Texas criminal history, period filmmaking and multiple in-state locations.

## 11. *Bernie* (2011)

East Texas towns and culture are essential to the film’s identity. The official Linklater Film Trail includes *Bernie* and can anchor a Carthage/East Texas travel connection.

## 12. *Boyhood* (2014)

Filmed over many years, *Boyhood* records changing Texas places as well as changing actors. It belongs in the guide because the production process itself turned Texas locations into a long-term visual timeline.

## 13. Texas is more than a Western backdrop

The Texas Film Commission’s filmography spans horror, comedy, drama, television, animation and other production types. A final article should resist presenting Texas filmmaking as only ranches and desert roads.

## 14. Build location trips from verified public places

For every film stop, label the site as:

- public attraction;
- public street/viewpoint;
- business with current visitor access;
- private property/not a visitor destination.

## 15. Keep the list expandable

The Film Commission’s filmography runs through 2025 and continues to grow. New productions should enter a current callout rather than forcing the evergreen historical core to chase every release.
