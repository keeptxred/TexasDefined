# Historic Texas Hotels With Famous Ghost Stories

Texas has plenty of historic hotels with long-running ghost stories, but a useful article should distinguish documented building history from paranormal claims that cannot be verified.

## How to frame the stories

Each entry should include the documented hotel history first: construction, major owners, restorations, notable guests and architectural significance. Ghost accounts can follow as folklore attributed to hotel tradition, local reporting or published legends.

## Good candidates share three traits

- A genuinely historic property or building
- A well-documented public history
- A ghost story that can be traced to a named source rather than copied from anonymous listicles

## Questions to answer for readers

- Is the hotel still operating?
- Is the historic building the same structure associated with the story?
- Are tours offered, or is access limited to registered guests?
- Does the hotel itself promote the story?
- Which details are documented history and which are folklore?

## Respect current guests and staff

Do not encourage readers to roam closed floors, enter service areas or disturb guests looking for paranormal evidence. Historic hotels are active businesses, not abandoned attractions.

## Internal-link plan

Link to city pages, historic architecture, ghost towns/legends, weekend trips and restored-building coverage.

## Verification notes

Verify current operation, ownership/name changes and public access directly with the property. Use THC/National Register documents and local archives for building history. Label supernatural claims as stories or reported experiences, never established facts.

## Image guidance

Use a rights-cleared exterior or historic image of a featured hotel. Do not reuse hotel marketing photography without permission.
