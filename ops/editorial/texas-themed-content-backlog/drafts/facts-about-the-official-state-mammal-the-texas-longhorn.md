# Facts About the Official State Mammal: the Texas Longhorn

The Texas Longhorn is tied to cattle drives, ranching history and the visual identity of the state, but the breed’s story is more complicated than the familiar silhouette suggests.

## 1. It developed in North America over centuries
Longhorn cattle trace to cattle brought by Spanish colonists and later populations shaped by feral survival and selective breeding in Texas and neighboring regions.

## 2. Horn shape varies widely
Not every Longhorn has the same dramatic lateral horn shape. Genetics, sex, age and breeding lines matter.

## 3. The breed is known for hardiness
Historical Longhorn populations survived heat, sparse forage, predators and long movement across difficult country.

## 4. Longhorns powered the post-Civil War cattle economy
Large trail drives moved Texas cattle north to railheads, helping connect Texas ranching to national markets.

## 5. The cattle-drive era was relatively brief
Popular culture can make trail driving seem like a timeless Texas institution, but its classic open-range period occupied a limited historical window.

## 6. The breed nearly disappeared from mainstream commercial production
As fencing, railroads and consumer preferences changed, other cattle breeds became more commercially dominant.

## 7. Preservation efforts mattered
Government, ranch and private breeding programs helped preserve distinct Longhorn populations and genetics.

## 8. Longhorn cattle remain working livestock
They are not merely mascots or heritage animals; ranchers continue to breed them for beef, genetics, exhibition and conservation purposes.

## 9. They are part of Texas symbolism
The Longhorn appears in tourism, universities, ranch brands and public art, but the livestock history should not be reduced to a logo.

## 10. Breed claims need precise sourcing
Claims about horn-span records, oldest bloodlines or exact ancestry can change or be disputed and should be verified before publication.
