# Famous Speeches Delivered by Texans

A useful Texas speech-history article should explain why a speech mattered, where it was delivered, who the audience was and how historians interpret it. It should not reproduce long copyrighted transcripts.

## Possible speech traditions to cover

### Sam Houston and Republic-era politics
Use authenticated legislative, diplomatic or campaign addresses to show how leaders argued about independence, annexation and governance.

### Barbara Jordan
Jordan’s national political speeches, including her 1974 House Judiciary Committee remarks and 1976 Democratic National Convention keynote, are major examples of Texas oratory with national impact.

### Lyndon B. Johnson
Speeches on civil rights, voting rights and the Great Society connect a Texas political career to national policy transformation.

### Ann Richards
Her public speaking style became part of modern Texas political culture, but any quoted material should be minimal and sourced.

### Military, civil-rights and cultural voices
The article should not be limited to elected officials; civic leaders, activists, veterans, educators and cultural figures can broaden the record.

## Copyright and quotation rules

Prefer paraphrase and context. Use only brief quotations when necessary to identify a historically significant phrase, and verify the transcript against an archive or official record.

## Internal-link plan

Link to civil-rights history, Republic history, Texas women, presidential history, state archives and city/county pages tied to speech locations.

## Source notes

Use presidential libraries, Congress, TSLAC, university archives, convention archives and authoritative transcript collections. Verify date, venue and wording independently.

## Image guidance

Use rights-cleared archival podium, audience or portrait imagery with documented date and source.
