# 10 Major Industries Driving the Texas Economy

Texas is often described as an oil economy. Energy remains enormously important, but that shorthand misses how broad the state's economic base has become. Manufacturing, logistics, technology, health care, construction, food production, aerospace and other industries interact with the energy sector rather than simply sitting beside it.

This article should use current employment/output data when published. The category list is durable; rankings and statistics are not.

## 1. Energy: oil, gas, power and renewables

Energy remains one of Texas's defining economic advantages. The state's energy sector includes oil and gas extraction, pipelines, refining, petrochemicals, electricity generation and transmission, wind, solar and supporting equipment/services.

Texas's current economic-development strategy groups these activities under an “Energy Evolution” sector rather than treating the economy as only hydrocarbons.

## 2. Advanced manufacturing and semiconductors

Texas manufactures everything from electronics to heavy machinery, vehicles and aerospace systems. State economic-development materials identify computers, electronics and semiconductors as a target cluster inside advanced manufacturing.

Semiconductor investment is especially important because Texas already has a deep history in electronics. Texas Instruments engineer Jack Kilby developed the integrated circuit in 1958, and new fabrication investment continues that lineage.

## 3. Transportation, logistics and distribution

Texas sits at the center of major interstate, rail, port, air-cargo and cross-border trade networks. Warehousing, distribution, trucking, aviation services and e-commerce therefore form a major economic cluster.

The state's geography matters: Texas connects Gulf ports, Mexico trade gateways and large internal consumer markets while serving as a national distribution hub.

## 4. Health care and life sciences

Health care is one of the largest sources of employment in Texas, while life sciences and biotechnology add research, pharmaceuticals, medical devices and commercialization.

Houston's medical complex is the best-known example, but the broader sector reaches Dallas-Fort Worth, Austin, San Antonio and university/research communities across the state.

## 5. Construction and real estate

Population growth and business investment create demand for homes, apartments, roads, warehouses, factories, data centers, utilities and commercial space.

Construction has therefore been a major job-producing sector, but it is cyclical and highly sensitive to interest rates, project pipelines and local housing conditions.

## 6. Aerospace, aviation and defense

Texas combines military installations, aerospace manufacturing, commercial aviation, spaceflight, defense contractors and research.

The sector stretches from North Texas aerospace/defense manufacturing to Houston's NASA-centered human-spaceflight ecosystem and newer commercial-space operations elsewhere in the state.

## 7. Technology, software and professional services

Texas's technology economy is broader than Austin. Software, cloud infrastructure, cybersecurity, data centers, engineering, computer-systems design, consulting and corporate management employment extend across several metros.

Comptroller data has highlighted strong growth in computer-systems design and related professional-services industries over the past decade.

## 8. Food, livestock and agriculture

Cattle, poultry, dairy, cotton, grains and specialty crops remain important, but the economic chain continues through food processing, cold storage, packaging, restaurants, distribution and export.

The state's target-industry strategy explicitly treats food and livestock products as an economic sector linked to manufacturing, life sciences and logistics.

## 9. Petroleum refining and chemicals

The Gulf Coast's refinery and chemical complex converts raw energy resources into fuels, plastics, chemical feedstocks and industrial materials used around the world.

This deserves its own category because downstream refining and chemicals are economically different from drilling a well in the Permian Basin, even though the sectors are connected.

## 10. Trade, ports and cross-border commerce

Texas's economy is inseparable from trade. Gulf ports handle energy, containers, vehicles, agricultural commodities and industrial cargo, while land ports along the Mexico border connect manufacturing and supply chains across North America.

Transportation, warehousing, manufacturing and retail all benefit from that trade infrastructure.

## The useful takeaway is diversification

Texas still has industry concentrations that can create regional booms and slowdowns. Midland responds differently to oil prices than Austin responds to technology hiring or Houston to a mix of energy, medicine, port activity and construction.

For people moving to Texas, choosing a city, or relocating a business, the state economy should therefore be analyzed region by region as well as statewide.
