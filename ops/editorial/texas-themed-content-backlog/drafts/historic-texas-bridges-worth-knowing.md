# 10 Historic Texas Bridges Worth Knowing

Bridges are some of the clearest evidence of how Texans solved geography. Rivers, bayous, canyons and railroad corridors determined where people could move, which towns became trade centers and how roads connected before the interstate era.

This guide focuses on bridges with strong historical or engineering stories. Current access matters: some historic bridges are pedestrian landmarks, some still carry traffic, and others are best understood from designated viewpoints rather than explored directly.

## 1. Waco Suspension Bridge — Waco

Opened in 1870, the Waco Suspension Bridge became a major Brazos River crossing and an important piece of the cattle-trail and commercial story of Central Texas.

Today its value is both engineering and place-making: the bridge helps connect downtown Waco, the riverfront and the city’s nineteenth-century growth.

## 2. Regency Suspension Bridge — Mills/San Saba county line

The Regency Suspension Bridge spans the Colorado River in rural Central Texas and has become one of the state’s most recognizable surviving suspension bridges.

Its remote setting makes it a natural fit for county and back-road content, but current vehicle/pedestrian access and structural status must be verified before publication.

## 3. Faust Street Bridge — New Braunfels

The Faust Street Bridge crosses the Guadalupe River and preserves an early twentieth-century highway-engineering story in New Braunfels.

It works especially well in a regional article because the bridge can be connected to German-Texan settlement, river recreation and Comal County growth.

## 4. Hays Street Bridge — San Antonio

San Antonio’s Hays Street Bridge is a reused metal truss bridge that became a pedestrian and neighborhood landmark.

The adaptive-reuse story is as important as the original transportation function: old infrastructure can become public space rather than simply disappear.

## 5. Ann W. Richards Congress Avenue Bridge — Austin

The current Congress Avenue Bridge dates to the early twentieth century and is now globally famous for the Mexican free-tailed bat colony beneath it.

That gives the bridge two stories at once—urban transportation history and wildlife adaptation in the center of a modern city.

## 6. Rainbow Bridge — Jefferson/Orange county area

The Rainbow Bridge over the Neches River became a major Gulf Coast engineering landmark because its high clearance allowed oceangoing ships to pass beneath.

Its scale reflects the industrial and maritime geography of the Beaumont–Port Arthur region rather than a picturesque small-town crossing.

## 7. Old Alton Bridge — Denton County

Old Alton Bridge is a nineteenth-century iron bridge preserved as a pedestrian landmark near Denton.

The bridge has accumulated folklore as well as documented transportation history. A responsible article should clearly separate archival history from ghost-story material.

## 8. Roy B. Inks Bridge — Marble Falls

The bridge at Marble Falls is part of the Highland Lakes/Colorado River infrastructure story and connects a city whose identity changed dramatically with dam construction and lake development.

Verify formal historic status, dates and current access against TxDOT/THC records before publication.

## 9. Pecos River railroad high bridge — Val Verde County region

The high railroad crossings over the Pecos became iconic images of transportation in rugged West Texas. Railroad engineering here had to solve canyon-scale geography far different from East or Central Texas river bridges.

Because active railroad infrastructure is dangerous and access may be restricted, this entry should be written as history/viewing context, not an invitation to trespass.

## 10. Historic international crossings in El Paso and Presidio

Texas border bridges carry layers of railroad, pedestrian, commercial and diplomatic history. Individual bridge names, operating status and access change over time, so this section should eventually be split into exact bridge profiles backed by federal/local records.

The larger point is that a bridge across the Rio Grande is not just engineering; it is infrastructure shaped by two countries and one border region.

## What historic bridges can add to county pages

For TexasDefined’s county system, bridges can supply concrete local history:

- first reliable river crossings;
- cattle and freight routes;
- railroad expansion;
- early highway systems;
- New Deal or twentieth-century public works;
- flood replacement and engineering change;
- connections between downtown development and transportation.

A county page with one verified bridge story is much stronger than a generic “transportation has always been important” paragraph.

## How to visit old bridges responsibly

Before a trip:

- verify whether the bridge is open to pedestrians or vehicles;
- obey posted weight limits and closures;
- do not enter active railroad property;
- do not climb bridge structures;
- use official parking/access points;
- check flood conditions at low-lying river crossings.

Historic does not mean abandoned or safe to explore without rules.
