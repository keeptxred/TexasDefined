# Castle-Like Structures You Wouldn’t Expect to Find in Texas

Texas has buildings that look like castles, but very few are castles in the European defensive sense. A strong guide should distinguish **historic residences, institutional buildings, revival architecture and purpose-built attractions** rather than label every turret a medieval fortress.

## 1. Bishop’s Palace — Galveston

The 1892 Bishop’s Palace is a major Victorian residence with dramatic towers and masonry. It is better described through its documented architectural history than as a literal castle.

## 2. Elisabet Ney Museum / Formosa — Austin

Sculptor Elisabet Ney’s studio has a fortress-like visual character and a strong link to Texas art history. Current museum access should be verified through the City of Austin.

## 3. Old Red Museum / Dallas County Courthouse

The former Dallas County Courthouse uses Romanesque Revival architecture, red sandstone and turrets that create a castle-like silhouette.

## 4. Pythian Temple — Dallas

The historic Knights of Pythias Temple is architecturally significant for reasons deeper than its monumental appearance, including Black civic and cultural history in Dallas.

## 5. Texas Military Institute / castle-like historic school buildings

Some institutional campuses used Gothic or fortress-like elements. Individual buildings should be included only where the structure survives and visitor access is documented.

## 6. Falkenstein Castle — Burnet County area

A modern private/event property inspired by European castle architecture. Current public/event access must be verified; do not imply visitors may enter private property casually.

## 7. Newman’s Castle — Bellville

A modern castle attraction near Bellville associated with tours/events. Current reservation requirements and access should be checked directly.

## 8. Castle Avalon — New Braunfels area

Texas has had modern castle-themed event properties whose names/operations can change. Include only after confirming current operating status.

## 9. Gothic Revival university buildings

Texas campuses contain towers, crenellations and Collegiate Gothic forms that can appear castle-like without being castles.

## 10. Courthouses with fortress-like Romanesque forms

Several historic Texas courthouses use towers, heavy masonry and arches associated with Romanesque Revival. Link to the dedicated courthouse guide rather than duplicate its list.

## 11. Mission and presidio architecture is not “castle architecture”

Spanish mission compounds and presidios can be fortified, but calling them castles erases their specific colonial, religious and military history.

## 12. Private fantasy architecture

Texas has eccentric private residences and event spaces built with towers, battlements and faux-medieval elements. Privacy and current access are more important than viral-photo value.

## What counts as a castle here?

Use three labels:

- **historic castle-like architecture** — real historic building with fortress/Gothic/Romanesque features;
- **modern castle-inspired structure** — intentionally designed to resemble a castle;
- **actual fortification** — military/defensive site that should be described by its real historical type, such as fort or presidio.
