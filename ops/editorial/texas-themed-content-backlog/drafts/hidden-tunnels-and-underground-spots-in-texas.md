# Underground Places and Tunnels in Texas You Can Explore Legally

Texas has caves, historic basements, pedestrian tunnels and underground infrastructure, but “hidden tunnel” lists often drift into private property, utility spaces or unsafe urban exploration.

This article should include only places with **verified legal public access or official tours**.

## 1. Natural Bridge Caverns

A major commercial cave system near San Antonio with guided public tours. Tour offerings and access requirements should be verified directly.

## 2. Caverns of Sonora

A privately operated show cave in West Texas known for highly decorated formations. Current tour schedules require annual review.

## 3. Inner Space Cavern

A Georgetown-area cave discovered during interstate construction and now operated as a public attraction.

## 4. Longhorn Cavern State Park

A state-park cave with guided tours and a layered history that includes geology, tourism and earlier human use.

## 5. Cave Without a Name

A privately operated Hill Country show cave. Current tour availability and events should be checked on the official site.

## 6. Cascade Caverns

A Boerne-area commercial cave with long tourism history. Access and tour format change and require current verification.

## 7. Houston downtown tunnel system

Downtown Houston has a large climate-controlled pedestrian tunnel network connecting office buildings and businesses. Public access is generally tied to weekday/business-hour conditions and building rules, not a 24-hour tourist tunnel.

## 8. San Antonio underground/river infrastructure stories

San Antonio has engineered flood-control and river infrastructure, but operational tunnels and utility spaces should never be presented as visitor attractions unless the managing authority explicitly offers access.

## 9. Historic mine sites with official interpretation

Texas has mining history, but abandoned mines are hazardous. Include only museums, parks or interpretive sites with explicit public access.

## 10. Archaeological/cultural caves with managed access

Some Texas caves contain important cultural or archaeological resources. Exact locations may be restricted, and the article should follow managing-agency disclosure rules.

## What this guide deliberately excludes

- storm drains;
- utility tunnels;
- railroad tunnels without public trails;
- abandoned mines;
- sealed basements;
- private caves;
- service corridors under buildings;
- locations shared only by urban-exploration forums.

## Why “hidden” can be misleading

If a place is safe and legal for visitors, it usually has an owner or managing agency. Truly hidden infrastructure is often hidden because it is dangerous, private, operational or protected.
