# 10 Texas Inventions and Innovations That Traveled Far Beyond the State

A useful list of Texas inventions has to be stricter than the viral versions. Some products were genuinely invented here. Others were developed, commercialized or transformed here even when the underlying idea came from somewhere else.

This draft separates those categories rather than claiming Texas invented everything it touched.

## 1. The integrated circuit — Texas Instruments, Dallas

In 1958, Texas Instruments engineer Jack Kilby demonstrated an integrated electronic circuit that placed multiple components on a single piece of semiconductor material. The breakthrough became one of the foundations of modern microelectronics.

Kilby later shared the 2000 Nobel Prize in Physics for work on the integrated circuit.

**Texas connection:** developed at Texas Instruments in Dallas.

## 2. The handheld electronic calculator — Texas Instruments

Texas Instruments engineers including Jack Kilby, Jerry Merryman and James Van Tassel developed an early battery-powered handheld calculator prototype in the 1960s.

The project helped demonstrate that increasingly complex electronics could move from desks and laboratories into portable consumer devices.

**Texas connection:** TI engineering and development in Dallas.

## 3. The first frozen margarita machine — Dallas

In 1971, Dallas restaurateur Mariano Martinez adapted a soft-serve machine to produce consistent frozen margaritas at restaurant scale. The Smithsonian now preserves the original machine and describes it as the world's first frozen margarita machine.

The important distinction: Martinez did not invent the margarita or frozen drinks. He invented a restaurant production system that changed how the drink was served.

**Texas connection:** Mariano's Mexican Cuisine in Dallas.

## 4. Liquid Paper — Dallas

Bette Nesmith Graham, a Dallas secretary and artist, developed a correction fluid after realizing typists could cover mistakes in a way similar to painters covering errors on a canvas.

Her product evolved from “Mistake Out” into Liquid Paper and became a major office-supply brand.

**Texas connection:** developed and commercialized in Dallas.

## 5. Commercial silicon-transistor development at Texas Instruments

Texas Instruments scientist Gordon Teal helped push semiconductor technology from germanium toward silicon in the 1950s. TI's silicon-transistor work became an important step toward more durable, heat-tolerant electronic components.

This topic requires precise final wording because multiple laboratories contributed to transistor and silicon-semiconductor history. TexasDefined should claim the documented Texas milestone, not “Texas invented the transistor.”

## 6. Selective laser sintering at the University of Texas at Austin

In the 1980s, UT Austin researcher Joe Beaman and student Carl Deckard developed selective laser sintering, an additive-manufacturing process that uses a laser to fuse powdered material.

The Texas Comptroller's 2026 R&D history highlights the work as an important early 3D-printing innovation now used in fields including aerospace and medicine.

**Texas connection:** University of Texas at Austin research.

## 7. Dr Pepper — Waco

Pharmacist Charles Alderton developed the soft-drink formula that became Dr Pepper at Morrison's Old Corner Drug Store in Waco in the 1880s.

A beverage formula is different from a mechanical invention, but it belongs in the broader Texas innovation story because the product spread from a local drugstore into a national brand.

**Texas connection:** Waco.

## 8. Fritos as a modern branded corn-chip product — San Antonio

Charles Elmer Doolin encountered a fried corn-chip product in San Antonio, acquired the recipe/equipment and built it into the Fritos business.

The careful version of the story matters: Doolin did not invent the ancient idea of frying corn products. His Texas contribution was developing and scaling the specific commercial product and company that became Fritos.

## 9. The modern Dallas ATM/automated-banking rollout

Dallas-area companies played a major role in early automated banking technology, including Docutel's development and deployment of cash-dispensing/ATM systems around 1970.

Because ATM priority claims vary by country and by whether “ATM” means cash dispenser, card system or networked banking terminal, final copy should identify the exact Docutel milestone rather than saying Texas invented every ATM.

## 10. The Astrodome as a stadium-engineering innovation

Houston's Astrodome was not the first roof ever built over a sports venue, but it transformed expectations for large-scale climate-controlled stadiums and drove innovations in indoor sports surfaces after natural grass struggled beneath the roof.

It belongs here as built-environment innovation rather than a patent claim.

## The bigger Texas pattern: invention plus commercialization

The strongest Texas innovation stories often combine several ingredients:

- research universities;
- engineering talent;
- energy and industrial demand;
- military/aerospace investment;
- entrepreneurs willing to commercialize an idea;
- large Texas markets that let a product scale.

That gives TexasDefined a richer angle than “10 random things invented in Texas.”
