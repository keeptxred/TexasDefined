# Things Texans Say All the Time

Texas speech varies by region, generation and community. This list should explain phrases people may hear without pretending every resident talks the same way.

## Phrases and habits to discuss

1. “Y’all” — a practical second-person plural used far beyond Texas too.
2. “All y’all” — often used when a speaker wants to emphasize the whole group.
3. “Fixing to” — meaning preparing or intending to do something soon.
4. “Might could” — a Southern/Texas regional construction expressing possibility.
5. Giving distance in time: “about two hours” instead of mileage.
6. Referring to major highways by local shorthand or number.
7. “The Valley,” “the Panhandle” or “the Hill Country” without adding “Texas.”
8. Weather shorthand such as “blue norther” in historical/regional use.
9. “Come on in” or “y’all come back” as hospitality formulas.
10. “Bless your heart” with meanings that depend heavily on tone and context.
11. Calling a soft drink “Coke” in some communities while others use soda or a brand name.
12. Pronunciations of town names that surprise newcomers.

## Editorial note

Use linguistic sources for regional-language claims. Avoid fake dialect spelling that makes speakers look unintelligent.
