# Texas Swimming Holes for Beating the Summer Heat

Texas swimming holes range from spring-fed pools to river bends and state-park access points. The useful version of this guide is not a permanent “best” ranking; it is a planning framework that tells readers what must be checked before getting in the water.

## What to verify before leaving home

- The site is legally open to swimming.
- Reservations or day-use permits are available.
- Water levels and flow are appropriate.
- Flood, bacteria, harmful-algae or other health advisories are absent.
- Parking and trail access are open.
- Local rules allow the flotation gear or activity you plan to use.

## Types of Texas swimming destinations

### Spring-fed pools
Central Texas springs can stay comparatively cool even in summer, but capacity controls and reservation systems can be strict.

### River swimming areas
Guadalupe, Frio, Blanco and other rivers change dramatically with rainfall and drought. A place that was swimmable last year may be shallow, closed or hazardous now.

### State-park water access
State parks often provide clearer rules, restrooms and managed parking, but closures can occur after floods, drought or infrastructure work.

### Coastal and reservoir swimming
These require different safety checks, including surf, wind, boat traffic and designated-swim-area rules.

## Safety matters more than the list

Never jump or dive into unfamiliar water. Flash flooding can occur far downstream from the storm that caused it. Children and weak swimmers need close supervision and properly fitted flotation where appropriate.

## Source and freshness notes

Before promotion, verify every site with TPWD, the managing city/county/river authority, NWS flood information and applicable public-health advisories. Remove any location that is closed, privately restricted or unsuitable for swimming.

## Image guidance

Use one rights-cleared image of a legal public swimming site. Do not geotag fragile or unofficial access points.
