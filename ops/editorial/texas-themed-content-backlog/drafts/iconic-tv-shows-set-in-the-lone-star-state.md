# Iconic TV Shows Set in Texas — and Which Ones Were Actually Filmed Here

Television makes the same mistake-prone distinction as movies: a show can be **set in Texas without being produced here**. A useful TexasDefined guide should label setting and filming location separately.

The Texas Film Commission’s official filmography and Film Trails provide a verification layer for productions that used Texas locations.

## 1. *Dallas*

Few television titles are more directly tied to Texas identity than *Dallas*. The Texas Film Commission includes the series on its Texas Classics Film Trail.

The Southfork association helped turn a fictional oil-family drama into a lasting North Texas tourism image.

## 2. *Friday Night Lights*

The television series transformed Central Texas locations into a fictional high-school-football world.

It belongs in the guide both as a Texas-set story and as a Texas production, and it can cross-link naturally into the state’s football-culture coverage.

## 3. *Walker, Texas Ranger*

The title alone made the state central to the show’s identity, but a serious guide should verify specific production locations rather than assume every scene was filmed where the story says it occurred.

## 4. *King of the Hill*

Animated Arlen is fictional, but the show’s suburban Texas details, speech, weather, football culture and everyday routines made Texas identity central to its humor.

Because animation has no conventional filming location, label it clearly as a Texas-set cultural portrayal rather than a Texas location-production example.

## 5. *Lonesome Dove*

This miniseries belongs in television rather than movie coverage and is part of the Texas Film Commission’s Classics Film Trail.

Its legacy offers a bridge between screen culture and Texas cattle-drive mythology.

## 6. *The Son*

Period television has used Texas landscapes and history to tell stories about ranching, oil and family power.

Verify the series’ in-state production locations against the Film Commission filmography before promotion.

## 7. *Fear the Walking Dead* — Texas-era production

Long-running series can move settings and production locations between seasons. If included, the article should specify which seasons or episodes used Texas rather than labeling the entire series a Texas production.

## 8. *Cruel Summer*

Modern television production also uses Texas for stories that are not built around traditional Western imagery.

This helps the article demonstrate how the state’s production economy extends beyond stereotypical Texas plots.

## 9. Reality and unscripted Texas television

The Texas Film Commission filmography includes unscripted productions such as *Lone Star Law*, *OutDaughtered* and *Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders: Making the Team*.

Keep these in a separate category so readers do not confuse scripted Texas settings with reality/documentary production.

## 10. Texas animation and game-production industries

The Film Commission’s production directory also includes animation, VFX and game-development categories. That broader production economy is worth a sidebar even though those projects are not “filmed on location” in the traditional sense.

## What the final page should label

For every show, use four fields:

- **Texas setting:** yes/no/partial;
- **Texas production:** yes/no/partial;
- **verified Texas locations:** named only when sourced;
- **visitor relevance:** public site, business, private property or no physical location.

That structure makes the article much harder to get wrong.
