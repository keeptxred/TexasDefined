# How Texas’s Lack of a State Individual Income Tax Actually Affects Residents

Texas does not impose a state tax on individuals’ net income. Since 2019, Article VIII, Section 24-a of the Texas Constitution expressly prohibits the Legislature from imposing one.

That does **not** mean Texans pay no state or local taxes, nor does it automatically mean every household pays less tax than it would elsewhere. The useful question is how the overall mix changes.

## 1. Paychecks do not include Texas individual income-tax withholding

Workers can still owe federal income tax, federal payroll taxes and taxes to another state in particular circumstances. Moving to Texas does not erase obligations tied to income earned elsewhere.

The final article should not provide personalized tax advice.

## 2. Property taxes become more visible in household budgeting

Texas has no state property tax, but cities, counties, school districts and special-purpose districts can levy local property taxes.

The Comptroller emphasizes that property tax is locally assessed and administered. Homebuyers should evaluate the exact taxing units attached to an address rather than use a statewide average as if it were a bill.

## 3. Sales and use tax matter more to the revenue mix

Texas imposes a 6.25% state sales/use tax, with local jurisdictions able to add up to 2 percentage points in combined local sales/use taxes where authorized.

A no-income-tax discussion that ignores consumption taxes is incomplete.

## 4. Housing choice can change the tax tradeoff dramatically

A renter, a homeowner in a high-tax school district and a landowner with qualifying open-space valuation can experience the Texas tax system very differently.

This makes the page a natural entry point to property-tax calculators and exact-address tools.

## 5. Homestead exemptions matter for eligible homeowners

Texas law and the Constitution provide residence-homestead exemptions, including school-district relief and additional provisions for some older or disabled homeowners.

Amounts and eligibility rules can change. Link to current Comptroller/appraisal-district guidance rather than hard-code every threshold in evergreen copy.

## 6. High earners may notice the income-tax difference more in raw dollars

Because Texas does not levy a state individual income tax, a household with high taxable income does not pay a percentage of that income to Texas as an individual income tax.

That does not by itself establish total tax savings; property, sales, insurance, housing and other costs still matter.

## 7. Retirees still need to look at the whole budget

“No state income tax” is often marketed to retirees, but a relocation comparison should include housing, property tax, insurance, health care, utilities and transportation—not just tax on retirement income.

## 8. Business owners face a different tax framework

Texas’s individual-income-tax prohibition is not the same as saying businesses pay no Texas taxes. Depending on entity and activity, franchise, sales/use, payroll-related and local property obligations can apply.

Link to the existing business-relocation draft rather than turning this article into business-tax advice.

## 9. Local tax differences can be large

Two homes with similar sale prices can have different tax bills because taxing-unit boundaries, exemptions and appraised values differ.

This is where county and address-specific tools can outperform generic “Texas taxes are low/high” content.

## 10. The constitutional rule makes the policy unusually durable

Article VIII, Section 24-a states that the Legislature may not impose a tax on individuals’ net incomes. Any article explaining the policy should cite the constitutional text itself, not only relocation websites.

## 11. No-income-tax comparisons need a defined household

A useful calculator should ask for income, home value/rent, household spending and location. Without those inputs, comparing “tax burden” across states can become misleading.

## 12. The best takeaway is a tradeoff, not a slogan

Texas’s tax structure shifts what households notice. Income taxation is absent at the state-individual level, while local property and state/local sales taxes remain important.

That is the frame TexasDefined should preserve.
