# Texas-Made Salsa Brands to Know — With Origin and Production Verified

Texas has no shortage of salsa brands, but “Texas-style,” “Texas-founded” and “made in Texas” are different claims. This guide should verify where a product is actually produced before calling it Texas-made.

## Brands to research for the promoted list

### Mrs. Renfro’s
A long-running Fort Worth company with a strong Texas identity. Current manufacturing and ownership should be confirmed directly.

### Julio’s
Regional chips, salsa and seasonings with Texas roots. Current production location requires verification.

### Kylito’s
A Texas-associated salsa brand that can be included only after current company/manufacturing confirmation.

### Sanderson Specialty Foods / Texas-Texas Salsa
A useful research lead for Texas-branded salsa production; verify current ownership, plant location and retail availability.

### Fischer & Wieser salsa lines
Fredericksburg-based specialty foods may include salsa products, but exact current lines should be checked.

### Small GO TEXAN producers
The Texas Department of Agriculture’s GO TEXAN ecosystem can surface smaller salsa makers, but participation alone should not substitute for checking manufacturing location and current sales.

## What to compare besides heat level

- fresh versus shelf-stable;
- roasted versus raw flavor profile;
- tomato versus tomatillo base;
- chile varieties;
- ingredient list;
- sodium/sugar where relevant;
- refrigeration after opening;
- whether the company is Texas-owned, Texas-headquartered or only Texas-founded.

## “Made in Texas” verification checklist

Before promotion:

1. check company contact/about page;
2. inspect current package manufacturer/distributor language;
3. verify production facility where publicly documented;
4. distinguish co-packing from brand headquarters;
5. date-label ownership/acquisition information.

## Why a brand may still be culturally Texas after acquisition

A Texas-founded brand can be acquired and still keep its original recipes, plant or identity. The article should tell that history honestly rather than pretending ownership never changed.
