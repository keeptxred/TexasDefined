# Marine Animals You Can Find in the Texas Gulf

The Texas coast is more than beaches. Bays, passes, jetties, marshes, nearshore water and the open Gulf support very different animal communities.

This guide should avoid implying that every animal is easy to see from shore. Some are common in bays, some offshore, some seasonal and some protected species that should be observed only from a distance.

## 1. Atlantic bottlenose dolphins

Bottlenose dolphins are among the most familiar marine mammals along the Texas coast and are often seen in bays and passes.

## 2. Kemp’s ridley sea turtles

Kemp’s ridleys are closely associated with Gulf conservation and Texas nesting beaches. Their endangered status makes respectful observation and current nesting-season guidance essential.

## 3. Green sea turtles

Green sea turtles occur along the Texas coast, including areas where seagrass and protected habitat support feeding.

## 4. Loggerhead sea turtles

Loggerheads are another Gulf sea-turtle species documented in Texas waters. Do not disturb nesting turtles, hatchlings or stranded animals.

## 5. Hawksbill sea turtles

Hawksbills are rarer in Texas than some other sea turtles and are federally and state endangered. Their presence belongs in a biodiversity guide, not a promise that visitors are likely to see one.

## 6. Bull sharks

Bull sharks occur in Texas coastal waters and can use bays and estuarine environments. Their presence is a reason for normal coastal awareness, not sensational “shark-infested” framing.

## 7. Blacktip sharks

Blacktip sharks are part of Texas Gulf ecology and are regularly documented by fisheries and coastal-science sources.

## 8. Great hammerheads

Great hammerheads occur in Gulf waters. Any handling, fishing or conservation-status claim should be checked against current TPWD regulations before publication.

## 9. Cownose rays

Cownose rays can occur in schools and are one of several ray species in Texas coastal habitats.

## 10. Atlantic stingrays

Stingrays use shallow coastal habitat and can bury in soft bottoms. Beach-safety advice should rely on current local/agency guidance rather than folklore.

## 11. Tarpon

Tarpon are large, powerful fish associated with Gulf and pass habitats and have a long recreational-fishing history on the Texas coast.

## 12. Red snapper

Red snapper are iconic offshore Gulf fish. Harvest seasons, bag limits and federal/state water rules are highly time-sensitive and should never be hard-coded into evergreen body copy.

## 13. Cobia

Cobia are large Gulf fish found around offshore structures, buoys and open water.

## 14. Goliath grouper

Goliath grouper occur in Gulf waters but are not an ordinary Texas catch-and-keep species. Conservation and harvest rules require current verification.

## 15. Sperm whales and other offshore cetaceans

The Gulf supports offshore whales and dolphins that most beach visitors will never see. Their inclusion helps explain how deep-water Texas Gulf ecology differs from bays and surf zones.

## Five sea-turtle species occur in Gulf waters

TPWD educational materials identify five sea-turtle species in Gulf waters. All are protected, and current reporting instructions for stranded or accidentally caught animals should come from TPWD/NOAA rather than from this evergreen draft.

## Wildlife-viewing rules that matter

- never feed dolphins or other marine wildlife;
- do not disturb sea turtles or nests;
- report stranded marine mammals or turtles through current agency channels;
- keep distance from wildlife hauled out, stranded or behaving unusually;
- check fishing rules before handling protected or regulated species.

## Image guidance

Use one rights-cleared Texas Gulf wildlife image from TPWD, NOAA or another source with explicit reuse rights. Avoid underwater stock photography that cannot be tied to Texas waters.
