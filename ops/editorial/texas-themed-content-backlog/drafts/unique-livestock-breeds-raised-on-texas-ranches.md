# Livestock Breeds You’ll Find on Texas Ranches

Texas ranching is much broader than the Longhorn. Climate, forage, markets and regional history have supported cattle, sheep, goats and specialty livestock across the state.

A useful breed guide should explain **why a breed fits a Texas production system** instead of treating unusual appearance as the only reason it matters.

## 1. Texas Longhorn

The Longhorn is the most recognizable Texas cattle breed and a living link to Spanish cattle, open-range history and adaptation to harsh conditions.

## 2. Brahman

Brahman cattle are valued in hot climates for heat tolerance and adaptability, particularly in South Texas and Gulf-influenced regions.

## 3. Beefmaster

Developed in South Texas from Brahman, Hereford and Shorthorn lines, Beefmaster cattle reflect deliberate breeding for practical ranch traits.

## 4. Santa Gertrudis

Developed on the King Ranch, Santa Gertrudis cattle combine Brahman and Shorthorn ancestry and are one of the best-known breeds with direct Texas development history.

## 5. Brangus

Brangus combines Angus and Brahman genetics, pairing heat adaptation with beef-production traits.

## 6. Hereford

Herefords became important on Texas ranches during the transition from Longhorn-dominated open-range herds to more managed beef production.

## 7. Angus

Angus cattle are widely raised in Texas, though the breed is not Texas-origin. Their inclusion shows how modern ranching combines local conditions with national genetics and markets.

## 8. Charolais and other Continental breeds

European beef breeds and crossbreeding programs are common parts of Texas cattle production, especially where ranchers target growth and carcass traits.

## 9. Dorper sheep

Hair sheep such as Dorper have gained attention in Texas because they reduce shearing needs and can fit hot, dry production environments.

## 10. Rambouillet sheep

Rambouillet sheep have deep ties to West Texas wool production and range systems.

## 11. Angora goats

Texas, especially the Edwards Plateau, has a long history of Angora goats and mohair production.

## 12. Spanish goats

Spanish-type goats have long histories in Texas and are valued for hardiness and brush-country adaptation.

## 13. Boer goats

Boer goats are widely used in meat-goat production and are common in modern Texas livestock operations.

## 14. American bison

Bison ranching exists in Texas, but bison should not be treated as ordinary cattle. Management, fencing, handling and markets are distinct.

## 15. Heritage and conservation herds

Some ranches maintain heritage cattle, sheep or goat genetics for cultural, conservation or niche-market reasons. Breed status and conservation claims require current registry verification.

## Why Texas has so much breed diversity

Texas spans humid coast, pine forest, prairie, Hill Country, South Texas brush, High Plains and desert. No single breed is ideal for every region.

Ranchers balance:

- heat tolerance;
- forage availability;
- parasites;
- drought;
- calving/lambing/kidding performance;
- temperament;
- market goals;
- labor requirements.

## Source / verification notes

- Texas A&M AgriLife livestock resources should support modern production claims.
- Handbook of Texas ranching/breed history: https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook
- Breed associations should support formal breed-development history and current registry claims.
- Avoid declaring one breed “best for Texas.” Suitability is operation- and region-specific.
