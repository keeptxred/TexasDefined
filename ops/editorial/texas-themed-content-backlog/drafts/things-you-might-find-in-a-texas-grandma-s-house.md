# Things You Might Find in a Texas Grandma’s House

This is nostalgia, not anthropology. It should present possible family memories across different Texas communities rather than claim every grandmother’s home looks the same.

## Possible details

- A family recipe box or handwritten cookbook
- Church, school, military or community photos
- Quilts, crocheted pieces or other handwork
- A pecan cracker or bowl of seasonal nuts
- Cast-iron cookware that has outlived several kitchens
- Bluebonnet, western, religious or local landscape art
- Newspaper clippings and obituaries saved for family history
- A porch chair positioned for conversation rather than decoration
- Holiday serving dishes used only a few times a year
- A drawer full of reusable bags, foil, rubber bands or twist ties
- Family reunion photos with handwritten names on the back
- A garden, bird feeder or potted plants suited to the region
- School-yearbooks or local-history books
- Food containers sent home with visitors
- A story attached to almost every object

## Editorial note

Include different ethnic, religious, rural and urban family traditions if expanded. Avoid implying age, gender or region determines taste.

## Internal-link plan

Link to Texas home architecture, food traditions, family history, quilting/crafts, gardening and rural/community traditions.
