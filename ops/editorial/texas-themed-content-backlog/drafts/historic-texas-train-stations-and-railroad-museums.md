# 10 Historic Texas Train Stations and Railroad Museums to Research

Railroads changed Texas by compressing distance. Towns that had depended on wagon roads, rivers or cattle trails could suddenly move people, mail, livestock and freight on national networks. Depots became front doors to communities, and in many towns the railroad determined where the business district grew.

Today, surviving stations range from active transportation buildings to museums, restored depots and adaptive-reuse landmarks. Because operating status changes, this article is intentionally written as a research guide rather than a promise that every museum is open every day.

## 1. Galveston Railroad Museum — Galveston

Galveston’s railroad museum occupies the former Santa Fe Union Station complex and preserves the island’s role as a major rail-and-port gateway.

The strongest angle is the connection between trains, immigration, cotton, the port and the rebuilding of Galveston after major storms.

## 2. Temple Railroad & Heritage Museum — Temple

Temple grew as a railroad town, and its former Santa Fe depot provides a direct way to explain how rail companies created jobs, routes and whole communities in Central Texas.

Pair the museum with Bell County history and the city’s continuing rail identity.

## 3. Interurban Railway Museum — Plano

Plano’s Interurban Railway Museum preserves a different kind of rail history: the electric interurban system that once connected North Texas communities before automobile dominance.

This is useful because “Texas railroad history” should include commuter and electric rail, not just steam locomotives and cattle shipments.

## 4. New Braunfels Railroad Museum — New Braunfels

A historic depot in New Braunfels connects German-Texan settlement history with the transportation networks that helped turn the community into a commercial and tourism center.

Verify current museum hours and exhibits before promotion.

## 5. Rosenberg Railroad Museum — Rosenberg

Rosenberg’s identity is inseparable from the rail junctions that helped create the town. The local railroad museum and Tower 17 story make Fort Bend County a strong place to explain switching, dispatching and the logistical side of railroads.

## 6. Texas & Pacific Depot — Marshall

Marshall became one of East Texas’s major railroad centers through the Texas & Pacific. Surviving depot and railroad-history resources help explain why the city developed as an industrial and transportation hub.

Current public-access and museum arrangements should be confirmed before publication.

## 7. Cotton Belt Depot — Grapevine

Grapevine’s historic rail district and Cotton Belt heritage connect depot architecture with modern heritage tourism.

This stop can feed a broader article about how towns reuse railroad infrastructure for festivals, excursion trains and walkable downtowns.

## 8. Brownwood railroad heritage district

Brownwood’s rail history is tied to multiple lines and to the growth of a West-Central Texas trade center. The local railroad museum/depot area provides an entry point into that story.

Because local institutional names and opening arrangements can change, verify exact public sites before the final draft is promoted.

## 9. Mineola depot and rail history — Mineola

Mineola developed as an East Texas railroad town and remains one of the communities where rail identity is still visible in the downtown fabric.

This section should connect historic depot preservation with modern passenger-rail service where current facts support it.

## 10. El Paso’s Union Depot

El Paso’s Union Depot is a major border-city transportation landmark whose architecture and history reflect long-distance passenger rail, western expansion and cross-border commerce.

Unlike a museum-only stop, it is valuable precisely because historic rail infrastructure can remain part of a living transportation network.

## What a depot tells you about a Texas town

When researching a county or small town, a surviving depot can reveal:

- why the town was founded where it was;
- which commodities moved through the region;
- where hotels and commercial streets developed;
- how migration and immigration reached the community;
- which competing railroads shaped local politics;
- why some towns boomed while bypassed communities declined.

That makes railroad history extremely useful for county-page enrichment.
