# Unwritten Rules of Driving on Texas Highways — and the Written Laws Behind Them

Texas drivers talk about “unwritten rules,” but several of the habits people describe as etiquette are actually backed by state law or TxDOT safety guidance.

The safest version of this article should separate three things clearly: **law**, **official safety guidance**, and **custom**.

## 1. Move right after passing

TxDOT says signs reading **“Left lane for passing only”** identify a pass-only lane and drivers should return to the right after safely clearing the vehicle they passed.

Do not turn “drive 20 mph over the limit in the left lane” into advice. The posted speed limit still applies.

## 2. Move over or slow down for stopped emergency and service vehicles

Texas’ Move Over or Slow Down requirements cover specified stopped vehicles displaying required warning lights. The exact covered vehicle classes and penalties can change, so this section must be rechecked against current TxDOT/DPS guidance before publication.

## 3. Do not camp beside an 18-wheeler

Large trucks have substantial blind spots and need much more stopping distance than passenger vehicles. TxDOT recommends avoiding truck blind zones, not cutting closely in front and leaving adequate room when passing.

## 4. Match speed to conditions, not ego

The posted limit is not a promise that the road is safe at that speed in rain, dust, fog, ice, congestion or construction.

## 5. Expect long distances between services in rural Texas

West Texas, the Panhandle, border country and ranching areas can have long stretches between fuel, food, medical care and cellular coverage. The practical custom is simple: do not pass a reliable fuel stop with an almost-empty tank because the map makes the next town look close.

## 6. A farm-to-market road is not automatically a slow country lane

FM and RM roads can function as local streets, rural connectors or busy regional corridors. Road designation does not tell you current traffic volume, shoulder width or speed conditions.

## 7. Watch carefully at highway frontage roads

Texas metro areas use extensive frontage-road systems. Newcomers can be surprised by merging traffic, turnarounds and businesses reached from the frontage road rather than directly from the main lanes.

## 8. Give motorcycles and bicycles real space

“Share the road” is not decorative language. Passing distance, lane position and visibility matter, especially on rural roads with narrow shoulders.

## 9. Do not race a yellow light through a giant intersection

Many Texas suburban arterials are wide. A late yellow can leave a driver exposed in an intersection much longer than expected.

## 10. Construction zones deserve extra attention

Texas grows quickly and highways are frequently rebuilt or widened. Temporary lane shifts, narrowed shoulders, changing exits and work crews make familiar roads unfamiliar.

## 11. Use rest areas before fatigue becomes a problem

TxDOT advises drivers to take breaks and specifically points to safety rest areas and travel information centers as places to stop. Fatigue is not something to “push through.”

## 12. Flashing headlights are not an official traffic-control system

Drivers sometimes use headlight flashes as informal communication, but signals between strangers can be misunderstood. Never rely on a courtesy flash when a traffic law, sign, signal or right-of-way rule controls the movement.

## 13. Do not treat pickup trucks as a separate legal category

Texas road culture may be full of pickups, but the same laws on speed, following distance, impairment, seat belts and roadway conduct still apply.

## 14. Weather can change the road faster than the forecast implies

Flash flooding, severe thunderstorms, dust, wildfire smoke, ice and extreme heat all create region-specific hazards. “Turn Around, Don’t Drown” should remain a hard rule around flooded roads.

## 15. Courtesy is not surrender

Letting a vehicle merge, leaving room for trucks and avoiding retaliatory driving costs very little. Aggressive driving turns ordinary congestion into a safety problem.

## Law vs. custom quick guide

### Law / official guidance
- obey posted speeds;
- follow left-lane/pass-only restrictions where posted;
- comply with Move Over or Slow Down requirements;
- follow work-zone controls;
- obey seat-belt, impairment and traffic-signal laws;
- never drive through barricaded or flooded roads.

### Custom
- top off fuel before sparse rural stretches;
- leave extra room around trucks;
- expect frontage-road navigation in major metros;
- assume construction can change a familiar route;
- use courtesy rather than territorial driving.
