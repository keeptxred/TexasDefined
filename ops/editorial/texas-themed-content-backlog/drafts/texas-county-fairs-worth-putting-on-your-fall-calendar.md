# Texas County Fairs Worth Putting on Your Fall Calendar

County fairs are one of the easiest ways to see how Texas agriculture, youth programs, rodeo culture, food, music and community identity overlap. But an event page becomes stale fast if it hard-codes one year’s concert lineup or ticket price.

This article should keep the durable character of each fair in the main copy and move current dates, admission and schedules into an annual refresh box.

## Gillespie County Fair — Fredericksburg

The Gillespie County Fair & Festivals Association bills its event as the “Oldest Continuous County Fair in Texas.” The 138th fair in 2026 was scheduled for August 27–30 with livestock shows, agricultural exhibits, carnival/midway attractions, horse racing, live music and other competitions.

Keep the historical superlative attributed to the Fair Association unless an independent methodology is added.

## Fort Bend County Fair — Rosenberg

The Fort Bend County Fair combines livestock, youth competitions, rodeo, barbecue, concerts and community programming. Its 2026 season ran from September 18 through October 4, with the main fair/rodeo calendar occupying late September and early October.

The current schedule also included a PRCA Rodeo, junior livestock programming, art, horticulture and accessible/sensory-oriented activities.

## Washington County Fair — Brenham

Washington County’s agricultural history makes its county fair a natural fit for a TexasDefined county/history cluster. Before publication, use the Texas fair’s official site rather than search results from similarly named fairs in other states.

## Guadalupe County Fair — Seguin

A Seguin/Guadalupe County fair entry can tie youth agriculture, livestock and rodeo traditions into the same county pages already strengthened by the July Fourth material.

Current dates and programming must be verified from the official fair association each season.

## Fayette County Fair — La Grange

La Grange gives the fair guide another strong courthouse-town and Central Texas heritage connection. A final version should distinguish livestock/agriculture programming from concerts and carnival attractions rather than presenting the event only as entertainment.

## Comal County Fair — New Braunfels

The Comal County Fair is a useful regional counterpoint to Fiesta and Hill Country tourism. Current parade, livestock, rodeo, carnival and concert details should be sourced to the fair association immediately before publication.

## East Texas fairs deserve representation

The final statewide article should include at least one current East Texas fair after verifying its official calendar. That prevents the list from turning into a Central Texas/Hill Country roundup simply because those events have stronger web footprints.

## Panhandle and West Texas fairs deserve representation too

Agriculture looks different across Texas. A statewide fair guide should deliberately include cotton, cattle, grain, sheep/goat or other regionally relevant programs from West Texas and the Panhandle when a current official fair source is available.

## What county fairs actually preserve

The most durable editorial value comes from explaining the functions behind the midway:

- 4-H and FFA projects;
- livestock breeding and judging;
- agricultural education;
- homemaking, horticulture and arts competitions;
- scholarships and youth auctions;
- rodeo and horsemanship;
- local music and food;
- county identity and volunteer organizations.

## Annual refresh checklist

Before a fall fair page goes live or resurfaces:

- confirm exact fair dates;
- confirm which days require separate rodeo/concert tickets;
- verify livestock-show and auction schedules;
- check parking/shuttle information;
- review heat/weather policies;
- verify accessibility and sensory accommodations;
- confirm carnival operating hours;
- remove performers or attractions that are no longer current.
