# Texas Bugs and Insects You’re Likely to Notice

Texas has enough climate and habitat diversity to support an enormous variety of insects and other arthropods. A useful guide should distinguish **ordinary nuisance species, beneficial insects, medically important species and spectacular native wildlife** instead of calling everything a pest.

## 1. Cicadas

Summer cicada calls are one of the most recognizable Texas soundtracks. Multiple species occur, and emergence patterns vary.

## 2. Fire ants

Imported fire ants are widespread in much of Texas and can create painful stings and serious allergic reactions in susceptible people.

## 3. Mosquitoes

Mosquito abundance varies with rainfall, temperature and standing water. Disease activity changes over time, so current DSHS guidance should control health-risk language.

## 4. Monarch butterflies

Texas is a major migration corridor for monarchs moving between Mexico and the central United States.

## 5. Gulf fritillaries

These bright orange butterflies are common in many Texas gardens, especially where passionvine grows.

## 6. Pipevine swallowtails

Another striking native butterfly tied to host plants and seasonal garden habitat.

## 7. Praying mantises

Mantises are predators, but not every mantis seen in Texas is native. Species identification matters before labeling an insect beneficial or invasive.

## 8. Green lacewings

Lacewing larvae feed on many soft-bodied garden pests and are important beneficial insects.

## 9. Lady beetles

Texas has native and introduced lady beetles. The group is useful in biological control, but blanket statements about “ladybugs” can hide species differences.

## 10. Tarantula hawk wasps

These large wasps occur in parts of Texas and prey on tarantulas. They are impressive and generally best admired from a respectful distance.

## 11. Cicada killers

Large solitary wasps can look intimidating but differ behaviorally from aggressive social wasps. Avoid disturbing nests and verify identification.

## 12. Paper wasps

Common around structures and eaves. They can sting defensively around nests.

## 13. Leaf-footed bugs

These plant-feeding insects are common in gardens and agricultural settings and can be mistaken for assassin bugs.

## 14. Wheel bugs and other assassin bugs

Predatory assassin bugs can help control other insects but may deliver a painful defensive bite if handled.

## 15. Texas leafcutter ants

In parts of Texas, leafcutter ants create conspicuous trails and large colonies while harvesting vegetation to cultivate fungus underground.

## Not every “bug” is an insect

Spiders, scorpions, ticks, mites and centipedes are often casually called bugs but belong to different arthropod groups. TexasDefined should keep that distinction clear while still linking readers to practical household and wildlife guidance.

## When identification matters

Identification is especially important when deciding:

- whether control is necessary;
- whether a species is protected or beneficial;
- whether a sting/bite has medical implications;
- whether a pest-management product is appropriate;
- whether a species is native or invasive.
