# Historic Texas Cemeteries Where Notable Texans Are Buried

Texas cemeteries are historical records in landscape form. They preserve family names, migration patterns, military service, epidemics, segregation, community institutions and the lives of people who shaped the state.

A responsible travel/history article should treat these places as cemeteries first and attractions second.

## 1. Texas State Cemetery — Austin

The Texas State Cemetery is the most obvious starting point for notable public figures and state history. Burial eligibility is governed by state rules, and the grounds contain governors, legislators, military figures, writers and other prominent Texans.

## 2. Oakwood Cemetery — Austin

Austin’s oldest municipal cemetery contains burials connected to early city and state history. Specific notable graves should be verified against city records before promotion.

## 3. Glenwood Cemetery — Houston

Glenwood is a major historic Houston cemetery associated with political, business and civic figures. Current visitor rules and office guidance should control any walking itinerary.

## 4. Forest Lawn / historic Houston cemeteries

Houston’s cemetery history also includes African American, immigrant, family and community burial grounds that should not be omitted in favor of celebrity graves alone.

## 5. Greenwood Cemetery — Fort Worth

Fort Worth’s historic cemeteries document the growth of Cowtown, business leadership, military service and civic life.

## 6. Pioneer Park / Dallas historic cemeteries

Downtown Dallas preserves historic cemetery space tied to early settlers and city development. Verify current access and interpretation before publication.

## 7. San Antonio historic cemeteries

San Antonio’s burial landscapes reflect Spanish, Mexican, German, military and civic history. A final list should select individual cemeteries only after confirming public access and documented notable burials.

## 8. Galveston’s historic cemeteries

Galveston cemeteries preserve maritime, immigration, epidemic and storm history as well as prominent island families.

## 9. State historic-site and battlefield cemeteries

Some Texas historic sites include graves or memorial burial areas tied to military and frontier history. Treat commemoration and confirmed burial locations as separate facts.

## 10. Family and rural cemeteries

Many of Texas’ most revealing cemeteries are small family or community grounds rather than famous urban sites. Access may cross private land and must never be assumed.

## Why cemetery designation matters

The Texas Historical Commission’s Historic Texas Cemetery program records locations and boundaries, raises preservation awareness and can support historical-marker work. THC reports more than 2,300 designated historic cemeteries.

## Cemetery etiquette

- visit only where public access is legal;
- obey posted hours and rules;
- do not touch fragile markers;
- never make rubbings where prohibited;
- do not enter private land without permission;
- keep pets and photography respectful;
- do not post exact locations of vulnerable graves if preservation authorities discourage it.
