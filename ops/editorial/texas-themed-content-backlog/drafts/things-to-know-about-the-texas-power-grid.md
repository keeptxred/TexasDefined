# 10 Things to Know About the Texas Power Grid

Texas electricity is confusing because several different systems are often collapsed into one word: “the grid.” ERCOT operates most of the state's power system, but it does not own the power plants, poles or neighborhood wires, and not every Texan is in ERCOT or in a competitive retail market.

This page should remain a practical explainer, not a political argument. Because market rules, grid resources, demand records and consumer options change, all statistics and current-program references must be rechecked before publication.

## 1. ERCOT serves most—but not all—of Texas

ERCOT says it manages the flow of electric power serving about 90 percent of Texas electric load. Its grid covers roughly three-quarters of the state's land area.

That still leaves parts of Texas connected to other interconnections and utility structures. A statewide article should never imply every Texas address works the same way.

## 2. Texas is served by three major interconnections

Public Utility Commission materials explain that Texas is the only state served by all three major continental U.S. interconnections: ERCOT, the Eastern Interconnection and the Western Interconnection.

Most Texans are in ERCOT, but this is why an El Paso-area household or another non-ERCOT customer may have a very different electricity setup from a Houston or Dallas household.

## 3. ERCOT is a grid operator, not your electric company

ERCOT is the independent system operator for its region. It balances supply and demand, operates wholesale-market functions, coordinates transmission access and administers retail switching in competitive-choice areas.

It is not the company that repairs the line behind your house, sends every retail bill or owns every generating plant.

## 4. Generation, transmission/distribution and retail are different functions

The PUCT breaks the industry into three broad functions:

- **Generation:** producing electricity.
- **Transmission and distribution:** moving electricity across high-voltage lines and then local poles/wires.
- **Retail:** selling electricity service to end-use customers.

Understanding those layers explains why a customer can choose a retail company but still have the same local delivery utility.

## 5. Retail choice depends on where you live

Many customers in competitive portions of ERCOT can choose among retail electric providers. Other Texans are served by municipally owned utilities, electric cooperatives or utilities outside ERCOT and may not have retail choice.

A “best Texas electricity plan” article therefore has to start with ZIP code/service territory rather than assuming one statewide marketplace.

## 6. The company selling the plan may not own the wires

In competitive areas, the retail electric provider handles the customer relationship and plan, while a transmission and distribution utility operates the poles, wires and local delivery infrastructure.

PUCT materials identify five major TDUs in the competitive ERCOT market: AEP Texas Central, AEP Texas North, CenterPoint, Oncor and Texas-New Mexico Power. Recheck the official list before publication.

## 7. Delivery charges are regulated even when the retail plan is competitive

Retail-plan pricing is only one part of a bill. PUCT-approved transmission and distribution charges can also materially affect the total.

That is why consumers should compare the Electricity Facts Label and full bill structure rather than an advertised cents-per-kWh headline alone.

## 8. ERCOT has to balance supply and demand continuously

Electric systems must keep generation and demand balanced in real time. ERCOT's control-room and market functions coordinate resources so the system stays within operating limits.

Demand can change quickly during extreme heat, freezes or large industrial-load swings. Generation availability, transmission constraints and reserves all matter.

## 9. “The Texas grid” changes as generation and demand change

ERCOT's current profile lists more than 55,000 miles of transmission lines and more than 1,460 generation units, but those figures are not permanent facts. New generation, batteries, transmission projects and large loads continue to enter the system.

Treat capacity and record-demand numbers as date-stamped data, not evergreen trivia.

## 10. Household electricity strategy starts with the service territory

For a homeowner or renter, the practical sequence is:

1. Identify whether the address is in a competitive retail area.
2. Identify the delivery utility or local utility/co-op.
3. If retail choice is available, compare plan structure—not only headline price.
4. Check contract length, cancellation terms, bill credits, usage tiers and delivery charges.
5. Model summer usage separately because cooling load can change the economics dramatically.

That makes the article a natural feeder into TexasDefined's moving, utilities and cost-of-living tools.

## ERCOT is not the same thing as retail deregulation

This distinction is worth repeating. A customer can be inside ERCOT and still be served by a municipal utility or cooperative without the same retail-choice structure. ERCOT's grid role and retail competition are related but not identical concepts.
