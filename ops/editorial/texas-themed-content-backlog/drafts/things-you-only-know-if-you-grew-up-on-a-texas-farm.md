# Things You May Know If You Grew Up on a Texas Farm

Texas farms differ enormously by crop, region, scale and family history. This should be a memory prompt, not a claim that all rural childhoods are the same.

## Familiar experiences in some farm families

- Weather forecasts mattering because work plans depend on them
- Learning the difference between helpful rain and badly timed rain
- Knowing which gate should be left exactly as you found it
- Understanding that equipment maintenance can determine the day
- Long drives to parts, feed, school or town
- Harvest season changing family schedules
- Watching commodity prices without being old enough to fully understand them
- Knowing irrigation, dryland farming and ranching are different systems
- Learning to identify snakes, insects and wildlife without treating all of them as threats
- Dust and mud both finding their way into vehicles
- Family meals happening around work rather than a fixed clock
- Early responsibility for age-appropriate chores
- School friends whose families grew completely different crops
- Knowing county roads and farm-to-market roads by memory
- Seeing drought, flood, freeze or hail affect income rather than only inconvenience

## Editorial note

Do not romanticize child labor, unsafe machinery practices or economic hardship. Include modern technology and diverse farm ownership, not only nostalgic family-farm imagery.
