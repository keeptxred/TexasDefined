# Texas Job Sectors Worth Watching — Without Pretending Every “Booming Job” Is Everywhere

Texas has a huge labor market, but statewide growth headlines can hide major regional differences. Health care demand in one metro, energy activity in another and logistics growth along a freight corridor do not create the same opportunities in every county.

Texas Workforce Commission labor-market data should be the source of record for current employment, wages and projections.

## Current 2026 snapshot

TWC’s June 2026 labor-market dashboard reported about 14.47 million seasonally adjusted nonfarm jobs in Texas, up roughly 177,900 from June 2025, a 1.2% year-over-year increase.

Those figures are a dated snapshot and must be replaced with the newest release before publication.

## 1. Health care and social assistance

Population growth and an aging population support demand across hospitals, clinics, outpatient care, home health and related services.

The important question is occupation, not merely industry. Nurses, technicians, medical assistants, administrators and support roles can have very different training and licensing requirements.

## 2. Construction and skilled trades

Population growth, manufacturing projects, energy infrastructure and housing create demand for electricians, plumbers, HVAC technicians, welders, equipment operators and construction managers.

Growth can be highly regional and cyclical. Current TWC metro/Workforce Area data should be checked.

## 3. Transportation, warehousing and logistics

Texas ports, interstates, border crossings, distribution centers and large metros make freight and logistics a major employment ecosystem.

This sector links directly to the interstate, truck-stop, port and airport content already in the backlog.

## 4. Energy

Oil and gas remain important, especially in the Permian Basin, Gulf Coast and other producing regions. Texas also has large wind, solar, transmission and power-sector workforces.

Do not describe “energy jobs” as one uniform category; occupations range from field operations and engineering to construction, grid operations and corporate services.

## 5. Advanced manufacturing

Semiconductors, aerospace, automotive, electronics, chemicals and other manufacturing projects can create concentrated regional demand.

A project announcement is not the same as permanent employment. Use TWC employment data and company filings after facilities are operating.

## 6. Professional, scientific and technical services

Engineering, software, consulting, design, accounting and other professional services cluster strongly in the major metros and around key industries.

Wage ranges vary substantially by occupation and experience.

## 7. Information and technology

Texas has large technology workforces in Austin, Dallas–Fort Worth, Houston and San Antonio, but tech hiring is cyclical. Avoid “X jobs are exploding” language based solely on corporate announcements.

Use current TWC/BLS occupational data.

## 8. Education

Public schools, colleges, universities and training organizations employ large numbers of Texans. Teacher demand, certification rules and vacancies differ by district and subject area.

Current TEA/TWC data should be used if the published article gets specific.

## 9. Government and public infrastructure

State/local government, utilities, transportation and public-safety roles are part of the Texas labor market even when “hot jobs” lists ignore them.

## 10. Hospitality and tourism

Travel, restaurants, lodging, entertainment and events create significant employment, especially in destination metros and seasonal regions.

These jobs often have different wage and seasonality patterns from professional or industrial sectors.

## How to research a Texas job move

Readers should compare:

- occupation-level wages;
- local openings;
- license/certification requirements;
- cost of housing;
- commute geography;
- industry concentration;
- long-term projections versus one-year hiring spikes.

A future TexasDefined job-market tool could combine TWC wage/projection data with county or metro cost-of-living and housing data.
