# Texas Defined Editorial Style

Texas Defined should sound like a smart, friendly Texas lifestyle magazine. Readers should feel guided by a well-traveled neighbor with good taste—not instructed by software.

## The voice

- Warm, confident and conversational.
- Specific rather than promotional.
- Curious about Texas without turning every sentence into a slogan.
- Useful without sounding bureaucratic or technical.
- Proud of Texas without exaggeration, chest-thumping or clichés.

## The reader promise

Every page should help answer at least one of these questions:

- Where should I go?
- What is worth my time?
- What should I know before I get there?
- What does it cost?
- How does living here actually work?
- Why does this matter to Texans?

## Navigation

Navigation labels should be short and immediately understandable.

Preferred:

- Explore
- Weekend
- History
- Moving Here
- Home
- Real Estate
- Guides
- Shop
- About

Avoid adding “Texas” when the site context already makes it obvious.

## Headlines

Headlines should feel written, not generated from a search query.

Prefer:

- The State Parks We’d Drive Across Texas For
- Where Friday Nights Still Matter
- Thinking About Calling Texas Home?
- Homes, Land and the Long View

Avoid:

- Best Texas State Parks Guide
- Texas Sports Information
- Texas Moving Resources
- Real Estate Data Directory

## Section labels

Use recurring editorial departments where they fit naturally:

- Editor’s Picks
- Worth the Drive
- Hidden Gems
- This Weekend
- The Texas Table
- Made Here
- Front Porch
- Then & Now
- Keep Exploring

Do not force a department label where plain language is clearer.

## Buttons and links

Actions should describe what the reader gets.

Prefer:

- Read the story
- Read the guide
- Keep exploring
- Take a closer look
- See what’s nearby
- Plan your trip
- Browse the shop
- Find it

Avoid:

- View
- Open
- Submit
- Learn more
- View all
- Open dataset
- Continue

## Internal linking is required for every new article

Every newly published editorial article must include useful internal links to other Texas Defined content. An article is not publish-ready until this has been checked.

Requirements:

- Include at least 2 contextually relevant internal links whenever suitable destinations exist on the site.
- Prefer 3–5 internal links for normal long-form evergreen articles when the links genuinely help the reader.
- Link to a mix of related articles and useful site destinations such as Explore pages, city or county pages, guides, tools, calculators, event pages, Home & Garden, History, Moving Here or other relevant sections.
- Use descriptive anchor text that tells the reader what they will find. Do not use generic anchor text such as “click here.”
- Place links naturally inside the story where the related subject is discussed rather than collecting unrelated links at the bottom merely to satisfy the rule.
- Do not link a phrase to a page that does not directly help explain or extend that phrase.
- When adding a new article to an existing topic cluster, also consider whether one or more older related articles should be updated to link back to the new article.
- Never create or guess a URL. Verify that the internal route exists before publishing the link.

The goal is to help readers continue exploring Texas Defined while strengthening clear relationships among related stories, guides, destinations and tools.

## Filters

Filters should sound conversational.

Prefer:

- What are you looking for?
- Where in Texas?
- What are you in the mood for?
- How far would you drive?

Avoid exposing database field names such as category, type, region ID or status unless there is no clearer choice.

## Empty states

Never blame the reader or show a dead-end system message.

Preferred search message:

> We couldn’t find a match. Try a town, park, lake or favorite Texas food.

Preferred section message:

> We’re still gathering the good stuff for this section. Check back soon.

Avoid:

- No results found
- Empty result set
- Nothing here yet
- Data unavailable

## Cards and metadata

Cards should answer why something is interesting before exposing metadata.

Reading time should appear as “About 6 minutes,” not “6 min read.”

Use labels such as “Worth the Drive,” “Editor’s Picks” or a meaningful subject tag instead of internal content types.

## Technical language

Do not use the following in public-facing copy unless the reader truly needs the term:

- dataset
- database
- platform
- production
- implementation
- browser-only
- pipeline
- schema
- entity
- input/output
- catalog
- internal
- feature
- resource directory

Translate the idea into reader language:

- dataset → facts, numbers, records or information
- directory → find a city, find a county or local contacts
- calculator inputs → what you’ll need
- calculator outputs → what you’ll get
- resource → guide, help or useful starting point

## Accuracy and trust

- Do not claim firsthand reporting unless it happened.
- Do not call something the best without defensible criteria.
- Use precise numbers only when sourced and current.
- Keep SEO titles descriptive even when the visible headline is more editorial.
- Never expose internal site architecture, ownership boundaries or development notes to readers.

## Final copy check

Before publishing, ask:

1. Does this sound like a person wrote it?
2. Would the label make sense without knowing the site structure?
3. Does the reader know what happens after clicking?
4. Is any technical language serving the reader—or merely describing the system?
5. Could this sentence appear in a good regional magazine without feeling out of place?
6. Does the article contain useful, verified internal links to related Texas Defined content?
7. Should any older related story be updated to link back to this new article?
