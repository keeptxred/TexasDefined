# TexasDefined universal river and river-basin authority standard (production gate)
Last updated: 2026-10-12. Owner: TexasDefined Travel & Outdoors Desk.

## Scope: every existing river page, every future river page
This policy is **not** restricted to any starter list. The implementation inventory currently includes every canonical river and river-basin guide found in the existing geography-profile fixtures and additional river-recreation guides. The two statewide river/basin pillar pages also remain required cross-link destinations. County, city, destination, lake, park, paddling, fishing, camping, RV and itinerary pages must display relevant reciprocal links when they share an actual geographic or visitor-planning relationship. This applies to existing and newly created content. Add new named waterways to the canonical registry once, rather than copying links into dozens of articles.

## Definition of a completed river guide
A river-wide article must be the best independent **visitor-planning and reference resource** we can factually support. It should ADD explanatory synthesis and comparisons to official sources, never duplicate the agency's content or claim to be a government authority. "Higher authority" means more useful, accurately sourced and maintained, **not** a guarantee of Google rankings or greater legal credibility than official authorities.

1. **Canonical identity.** One stable /article/texas-<river>-river-guide URL or the established /article/texas-<river>-river-basin-guide URL; no competing duplicates. Identify river headwaters, course, tributaries, basin, reaches and where it meets other waters. Distinguish reservoir, tributary and city float reaches.
2. **River-specific recreation.** Cover only applicable activities: fishing species and habitat, TPWD waterbody regulations and consumption advisories, legal public bank/boat access, mapped launch/take-out points, paddling or tubing suitability, river-level and flood safety; land travel and viewpoints for unsuitable remote rivers. Never force tubing or camping onto rivers where those activities are impractical.
3. **Actual visitor logistics.** Give managing authority and source links for access, operating notices, parking, permits, fees, toilets, changing areas, launch ramps, shuttle/exit arrangements, accessibility, campsite categories, overnight restrictions and local rules **when relevant and verified**. Unknown amenities must not be invented.
4. **Fishing rigor.** Fishing license exemptions at *specific* state-park boundaries, waterbody exceptions, catch limits and seasonal stocking are different concepts. Check regulation year (2026–2027), state park rules, TPWD tables and advisories. Avoid undated quantitative catch claims.
5. **Maps and originals.** Ground route maps in official geospatial data; no schematic presented as navigational. Use original/right-cleared real river imagery with creator, license and attribution where available. A photograph of a nearby reservoir or an illustration must be explicitly labeled rather than falsely captioned as the river. Generic imagery is a visible release-quality gap to resolve.
6. **History, science and ecology.** Distinguish documented Indigenous history, later settlement, dams, aquifers, riparian habitat, protected plants and water quality. Each special claim has attributable primary sources.
7. **Research FAQs.** At least five substantial river-specific answered questions informed by agency FAQs, local visitor questions and independent research; don't merely paste agency FAQ copy.
8. **Bidirectional links.** Add relevant upstream/downstream river, tributary, lake, fishing, camping, state-park, city, county, destination and itinerary links only where meaningful. Update reciprocal links at existing pages. Do not link every river to every river.
9. **Affiliate integrity.** Add contextually relevant, disclosed lodging/experience links only when actual providers and tracked links are verified. Never substitute an unrelated affiliate recommendation or let sponsorship affect factual ratings.
10. **Freshness and ownership.** Visible author/editor, source URLs, last research date, last *successfully editorially verified* date only when true, stable citation, and registry enrollment in the existing public.td_authority_verification_registry. Fishing regulations, park rules, leased access, hazards and prices: at most 30-day review cadence. Slow-changing histories: quarterly maximum. An automated unchanged HTTP fingerprint is not proof the factual content was checked.

## Publishing acceptance
No draft placeholder, inaccurate image, unlicensed photograph, expired lease promoted as open, fabricated facilities, unsafe access advice, unsupported price or disconnected page. The entire set must pass registration, river-coverage integrity, type/build, link, image, source verification and actual production browsing. If a required official access/site fact cannot be verified, label it unknown and give the live agency link. Production certification is withheld until all acceptance checks pass.

## Automation and future river requests
For every newly published **river or river-basin** guide, use the structured contract in `src/data/fixtures/texas-river-recreation-guides.ts` (or a substantively equivalent future contract), with named mapped visitor access, activity-specific advice, researched FAQ answers, authentic rights-cleared images, official sources and a river review schedule. No exceptions based on river size, region or order in which it was requested. Add the lazy stub and renderer, then register the canonical river slug and relevant county/destination slugs once in `src/data/river-authority-relations.ts`. The statewide hub, statewide river pillar, river-valleys collection, county-series articles, destination visitor-planning modules, and editorial article route now generate relevant reciprocal links automatically without duplicate hrefs. The article route links by exact river references in the title, summary and tags, not by an incidental mention buried in the article body. Relevance matters more than indiscriminately inserting every river into every guide. Unregistered future river guides fail validation. Run `npm run river:authority:validate` as part of the required `data:validate` gate. Register canonical route and sources with Supabase's existing td_authority_verification_* system with subject-appropriate cadence. If a new river is added to the geography fixtures without a comprehensive recreation spec and registry entry, validation MUST fail. Existing basin profiles are **not grandfathered**: each one has a dedicated reach-specific enrichment, is included in the shared registry and must pass the same content and source rules. Full live editorial certification still requires independent review of every assertion, photo rights, local access and operating details.

## Coverage, recurring source checks and remaining quality evidence
The canonical river inventory is dynamically audited against the two geography-profile modules plus the additional river-recreation module; this means the expected set grows as published river fixtures grow, and is not capped at an arbitrary number. The repository currently contains 15 dedicated geography/basin river guides and five additional stand-alone visitor river guides (20 in all). This number describes existing site coverage only, NOT every named Texas river or a limit on future pages. The registry is the single source for contextual county/destination reciprocity, river hub, river valleys and statewide pillar discovery. A published entry must exist in the registry and must have corresponding detailed content; hiding an incomplete page is not an acceptable workaround.

The Supabase `td_authority_verification_registry`, `td_authority_verification_sources` and scheduled jobs maintain per-page reviews. Currently registered river/basin guides are assigned a 30-day cadence; future `river-guide` and `river-basin-guide` sitemap URLs auto-register to that cadence. Operational data changing faster (flooding, closures, temperatures, releases, park reservations and fishing regulations) requires live source verification on each trip, and event-triggered review when notices change.

## Known evidence gaps at introduction
Rights-cleared true-river photographs for Comal, San Marcos and Pecos have been selected with explicit Commons attribution; they are currently loaded from the original Wikimedia host and should be cached as first-party optimized assets during final production acceptance. All existing river and river-basin geography guides have been expanded in the publication layer without overwriting the original basin research. Route-level amenities, fees and commercial campground inventory remain conditional and must be independently checked. These are not grounds to claim an award, a ranking, or total superiority to every official river authority.
