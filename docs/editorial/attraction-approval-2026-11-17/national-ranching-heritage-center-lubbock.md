# National Ranching Heritage Center: Historic Ranch Buildings and a Lubbock Visitor Guide

**Editorial status:** Development draft; owner approved development only, not publication.

## Why visit
The National Ranching Heritage Center in Lubbock makes ranching history tangible by combining an indoor museum with the outdoor Foy Proctor Historical Park. Visitors can compare structures, landscapes and interpretation across more than two centuries of ranching history rather than treating ranch life as a single period or a single architectural style.

## Explore the center
**Foy Proctor Historical Park.** The operator describes a **19-acre** outdoor historical park with **55 historic structures** arranged chronologically to illustrate ranch life from the late 1700s through the mid-1900s. The operator says **30 structures are between 100 and 177 years old**, with **1.5 miles of paved pathways**, bilingual interpretive signs and an optional phone app with audio, text and photographs. These concrete features should anchor the eventual route-by-route guide. Plan this part of the visit around heat, wind and mobility: it is not equivalent to walking through a climate-controlled museum. Take time to examine how historic structures illustrate work, settlement and the material demands of ranching.

**Indoor exhibits.** The operator describes a **44,000-square-foot museum with seven galleries** and 42 life-size bronze outdoor artworks. Use the galleries to connect the buildings outside to broader questions of livestock, ranch labor, technology and regional change. Temporary exhibit availability can vary, so check the operator's current exhibit listings rather than promising a named installation.

**Families.** The Cash Family Ranch Life Learning Center includes Hank the Cowdog's M-Cross Ranch. Verify children's program dates and age suitability through the operator; not every special event runs daily.

## Visiting information verified from operator
- Address: **3121 4th Street, Lubbock, TX 79409-3200**.
- General admission: **free**, with donations appreciated.
- Hours listed by operator: **Monday–Saturday 10 a.m.–5 p.m.; Sunday 1–5 p.m.**
- Closed on Texas Tech University holidays.
- Operator describes the site as wheelchair- and stroller-accessible, permits service animals only, and says no full-service restaurant is on site (snacks and drinks available at its shop). Accessibility needs should still be confirmed with staff for particular outdoor structures.
- Check operator event calendar and weather before travel; do not represent dated weekly or seasonal events as permanent.

## Suggested itinerary
Begin in the indoor museum to orient yourself to the period and themes, then explore the historical park when weather allows. Families may prioritize the learning center; visitors interested in architectural history may spend more time comparing historic ranch buildings. Pair with other Lubbock museums only after checking their own opening hours.

The operator asks all groups **larger than 20** to reserve a visit **at least two weeks in advance**, even when self-guided; groups of 20 or fewer need no self-guided reservation. Separately, groups of **25–50** may request a scheduled guided tour at least two weeks ahead, subject to staffing, with a **$50 group tour fee** (reservation confirmed upon payment). This is distinct from free general admission. That group-tour page also displays inconsistent gallery and park hours in its lower footer; prefer the operator's dedicated current visit page for general hours, and confirm group-tour logistics directly.

## FAQs
**Is admission free?** The operator currently lists free admission; special programs may have different arrangements and should be checked separately.

**Is everything indoors?** No. The outdoor historical park is a central part of the experience.

**Is the site stroller-friendly?** The operator states wheelchair and stroller accessibility, but ask about individual historic structures.
