## Shiner Lady Comanches — a championship program rooted in a changing school campus

Shiner's softball history should be told through verified coaching eras and UIL tournament records, not as a list of eight trophies. The [2015 UIL championship-era team sheet](https://www.uiltexas.org/softball/state-team/shiner-2014-2015-girls-softball) names Steven Cerny as head coach and John Hoelter as assistant. A separate [2016–17 UIL team record](https://www.uiltexas.org/softball/state-team/shiner-2016-2017-softball) still identifies Cerny, with Hoelter and Ray Neal. By the [2023–24 UIL tournament submission](https://www.uiltexas.org/softball/state-team/shiner-2023-2024-softball), Jason Keller led the Lady Comanches, assisted by Anastacia Rehak and Patrick Walker. Shiner ISD's [current softball page](https://www.shinerisd.net/athletics/softball) additionally lists Kailey Moeller in its coaching group. Those sources support a genuine coaching chronology but do **not** yet identify the exact hire and departure dates for the transition, which must be independently researched rather than invented.

The program's competitive trajectory also requires separate season and historical labels. The [2024–25 UIL school team record](https://www.uiltexas.org/softball/state-team/shiner-2024-2025-softball) documents the team's 14th state-tournament appearance, while 2025 ended with Shiner as runner-up rather than champion. The [2025–26 UIL submission](https://www.uiltexas.org/softball/state-team/shiner-2025-2026-softball) records 15 appearances, an additional championship, and the older title-marked years. These are snapshots submitted ahead of tournament results, not a fully updated final scoreboard. Do not reassign a submitted prefinal season record as the finished win–loss total.

The underlying institution has its own traceable history. Shiner ISD's [history](https://www.shinerisd.net/about-shiner-isd) describes school construction in 1890, replacements in 1911 and 1928, the new 510 County Road 348 campus beside Comanche Stadium in 2007, a junior-high addition opened in 2018 and further bonded school improvements in the 2020s. That chronology is useful local context for families following the Lady Comanches; it does **not** establish when girls' softball began or identify the current softball diamond. Even the Comanche Stadium proximity should not be used to provide an unsourced softball spectator entrance, parking lot or accessible path.

**To finish this page:** the actual varsity softball home field and precise public access; the Cerny–Keller transition timeline; sourced championship season-by-season finals and all-tournament honors; the authentic school-specific visual asset with permission; published 2027 schedule or explicit official schedule source; unique community reporting and credible original story sections.

## Pearland Lady Oilers — the school identity and the softball program are different histories

Pearland High provides unusually strong primary evidence for a genuine local-identity section. Its [own campus profile](https://phs.pearlandisd.org/our-school-phs/campus-profile/about-pearland-high-school) states the school was founded in **1937–38**; calls its overall mascot **Derrick**; identifies maroon, white and accent black as school colors; and records the school yearbook as *The Gusher*. It also publishes the campus fight song and a separate school song. An authority page can explain that these traditions belong to the whole school and give the Lady Oilers their institutional setting. It must not claim that 1937–38 was the start of varsity girls softball, that Derrick is a softball-specific mascot, or that a school song is a unique softball-team ceremony.

The softball story is independently dated. [Pearland ISD's June 3, 2026 championship report](https://www.pearlandisd.org/news/isd-news-details/~board/pearland-isd-news/post/pearland-oilers-capture-fourth-uil-6a-division-1-state-championship) documents the 6–3 title victory over Waco Midway and lists state softball championships in **1996, 2010, 2023 and 2026**. It identifies eight state-tournament appearances through 2026, showing four other runs that advanced that far without ending with a championship. Do not silently convert school-wide years of operation into extra volleyball or softball title history. The school's [athletics general-information page](https://phs.pearlandisd.org/athletics-phs/general-information) spells its girls athletics coordinator's name **Michele Hyden**, whereas the 2026 district championship article uses **Michelle**. A source-led profile should prefer the current official staff directory for her present name and retain the spelling difference as an editorial note rather than splitting one coach into two people.

Practical visitor information is a separate factual layer. The official school [softball ticketing page](https://phs.pearlandisd.org/athletics-phs/schedules-ticketing-information/softball) still shows May 2026 playoff games at a College Station site, including an apparent Game 3 opponent inconsistency. That is a useful demonstration of why a historic event listing must **not** become the permanent home-field address, a 2027 game schedule or a current opponent. Pearland ISD's [summer athletics page](https://www.pearlandisd.org/departments/athletics/summer-camps) lists youth softball activities through the school's athletic department; these should be distinguished from the **separate Pearland Power youth softball organization**, whose championships and fields are not Pearland High varsity records.

**To finish this page:** the named school varsity softball diamond and actual visitor access; substantive historical articles for the four title eras; dated coach biographies and alumni achievements; independently checked ticket/parking/ADA guidance; verifiable school-owned camp and eligibility links; licensed team/field photograph and reviewable rights.

## Katy Tigers — the three-title dynasty and the school district's living memory

Katy High's softball accomplishments extend beyond the current team's scoreline. An [October 2025 Katy ISD Hall of Honor announcement](https://www.katyisd.org/news-details/~board/news-headlines-katy-independent-school-district-100/post/champions-to-allstars-katy-isd-athletic-hall-of-honor-inducts-class-of-2025) explicitly inducted the **2015 Katy High softball championship team** as an entire team and separately listed **Coach Kalum Haack** among individual honorees. This is first-party evidence that the institution preserves team achievements in an athletic heritage program. The same announcement also recognized a football championship team and many other individual athletes: none of those should be presented as belonging to the girls' softball roster without sport-specific verification.

The [Katy ISD 2026 championship release](https://www.katyisd.org/news-details/~board/news-headlines-katy-independent-school-district-100/post/softball-dynasty-katy-softball-dominates-to-net-third-state-title-in-11-years) documents the win over Forney, the program's third 6A title in **2015, 2019 and 2026**, and a **39–5** season record calculated after the final. Earlier UIL submissions list a **38–5** record, representing a different reporting cutoff. The future page must explain that difference in context, with both source dates. It must also distinguish the archived form of the head coach's name from Katy High's newer staff directory, which displays *Megan Vanetta*. Those decisions protect individual identity and archive integrity rather than choosing whichever spelling ranks highest in a search result.

A historical home field must likewise be distinguished from a newly named one. The district's [July 27, 2026 board-meeting video index](https://www.katyisdtx.new.swagit.com/videos/394549) records **agenda item 9.11** concerning naming part of Katy High. That primary agenda proves the proposal was discussed but does not, alone, prove a final naming vote or a physical facility sign. Secondary reporting describes a vote honoring Coach Kalum Haack, whose name is independently supported by Katy ISD's Hall of Honor. Final field naming should be stated only after viewing the board decision/minutes and confirming the official form of the name. An older school athletics fixture calls the field *KHS-Softball Field*, a useful historic location label but no substitute for current gate/access verification.

**To finish this page:** official board minutes confirming the adopted softball field name; dated coach succession and third-title historical reporting; precise public softball stadium entrance and venue access; original local community/alumni narrative; validated spring-2027 team schedule source and a licensed photo.

## Ganado Maidens — a Texas Legislature-recognized first championship and the next season

Ganado's 2025 state title has a rare primary document that adds reliable game and community context: [Texas House Resolution 1528, adopted June 2, 2025](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HR01528F.htm). The House commemorates the **first UIL state softball championship in Ganado school history**. It records a 10–0 postseason run, a narrow **5–4** state final against Riesel and a **33–5–1** record across that completed 2024–25 season. The legislative resolution also identifies 2025 coach James Harp and his assistants. Because it is a dated government record celebrating that particular season, its game figures and staff should not be presented as the 2026 repeat title's record or as an automatic spring-2027 coaching assignment.

The accomplishment belongs to Ganado's Maidens, not the Ganado town's other sports, and it should be narrated as the start of a new championship era rather than implying a decades-long UIL state-title streak. The team's 2026 second consecutive title is documented separately in official softball tournament archives. A 2026 historical competition record may show a different win–loss count or group of assistants; that does not invalidate the legislature's 2025 record. It simply refers to a new softball season. A finished authority page can lay out a season-by-season title table with exact conference/division labels and correct awards instead of collapsing the evidence into a one-line “back-to-back champions” assertion.

[Ganado ISD's own campus directory](https://www.ganadoisd.org/campus) distinguishes **Ganado High School at 501 West Devers** from the district office at **210 South Sixth Street**. The campus directory alone does not tell spectators which softball gate to use or where cars can park. The [official Ganado High calendar and school homepage](https://ghs.ganadoisd.org/) use the name *Ganado Softball Field* for historical contests, but neither it nor the campus address automatically establishes a separate physical diamond location, current spectator access or accessibility features.

**To finish this page:** exact Ganado Softball Field footprint and visitor rules; independent 2026 championship reconstruction; older program history and other playoff seasons; genuinely school-specific community traditions; coach role verification for the forthcoming season and approved local photography.

## Amarillo Highland Park Hornets — establishing a real program without borrowing Dallas facts

The most important piece of research for the Amarillo Highland Park page is **entity identification**. The actual Amarillo Highland Park ISD operates at [hpisd.net](https://www.hpisd.net/) and lists a district address on Amarillo Boulevard East. Its 2026–27 UIL softball alignment record must be connected to **this Potter County Hornets campus**, not the Dallas-area Highland Park Scots, whose similarly named district uses a different website and different school records. A coach, field, bond document or historical record found under “Highland Park” without an independently matched campus would be dangerous to publish.

The [Amarillo district's live-feed archive](https://www.hpisd.net/live-feed?page_no=27) supplies a legitimate starting point for the girls softball program history. Among late-2024-era school announcements are a softball family meeting and an announcement promoting fan gear for softball and baseball as two recently added school programs. This is valuable school-issued evidence of a newer sports initiative. Yet the archive labels its items by relative age rather than complete publication dates; a careful article can describe these announcements but **cannot assign an exact first varsity softball season** without a dated board action, initial official schedule or district notice. It also cannot treat third-party initials for a coach as a confirmed first and last name or current position.

The same principle applies to facilities. A district bond plan that discusses softball improvements or a conceptual location south of the football facility is not evidence of an as-built field, actual address, opened spectator gates or ADA compliance. The right narrative here would explain how the program was introduced, the school system's support for it, its official UIL alignment and the milestones documented by original district/league reporting. Until that history and the current-field facts are genuinely source-checked, a full team page would be premature, and copying rich history from Dallas Highland Park would be factually worse than leaving it unpublished.

**To finish this page:** complete official 2024–27 program-launch timeline, current coach/staff and archived first season; as-built softball facility with access, image permission and actual field-specific map; independently validated results and history; original Hornets-specific local reporting.

## Shared publication boundaries

- **No false 2027 certainty:** UIL 2026–27 statewide district alignment and calendar are separate from an individual school's final varsity schedule. Last year's coaches and venue labels are historical unless corroborated in current school records.
- **No automatic athlete profiles:** State tournament records and public honors can be linked as sources; do not reproduce student contact details, unofficial recruiting information or unlicensed minors' photographs.
- **School vs. sport:** A school founding date, mascot, fight song, bonds, campus gym or football stadium must never be stated as the softball team's founding date, rivalry, venue or award.
