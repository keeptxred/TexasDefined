# TexasDefined — full lake authority baseline audit

**As-of review:** 2026-10-12  
**Repository:** `keeptxred/TexasDefined`, feature branch `feature/stamford-sweetwater-lake-authority-20261012`.  
**Evidence class:** repository-wide structural/content inventory with an independent primary-source benchmark. **Not** a claim of manual verification at every lake, operating facility, URL or image in production, nor a claim of outranking its owner.

## Executive conclusion

The full registered fishing-guide network contains **52 complete lakes** (1 dedicated Lake Conroe implementation, 14 showcase entries, 37 statewide-template entries), each with overview + eight intent section routes, yielding **468 lake URL surfaces**. There are **36 additional legacy lake/park/NRA destination records** in a separate discovery catalog (34 state-park destinations plus two federal national-recreation destinations). These may overlap the lake guides and should **not** be treated as 36 additional authoritative fishing lakes.

The benchmark is much larger: [TPWD's alphabetical freshwater lake directory](https://tpwd.texas.gov/fishboat/fish/recreational/lakes/lakelist.phtml) states **more than 150** lake profiles/access guides and provides an alphabetic list with approximately 175 linked entries, including park and alias entries. The lists do not correspond one-for-one, but the gap is substantial. TexasDefined is not yet a credible claim of being the authoritative source for *every* Texas lake.

Current evidence inventory after TPWD access review: **52 of 52** complete lake guides have at least one named facility (**140** TPWD-listed sites across the 37 statewide-template guides), **34** have a named overnight option, and **18** have no individually identified campground in the audited record. Named facilities do not imply a complete inventory or same-day operational confirmation. Source-specific history evidence is separately tracked; a specifically identified TWDB history/source link (not proof of a complete authored history); **47** lack such a source reference in the reviewed record; and **52** have image mappings (these are not newly commissioned TexasDefined photos). The site has a general Google Maps embed, not operator-verified ramp pins, bathymetry or boating-navigation charts.

**Critical content integrity:** Existing titles and SEO descriptions sometimes called general inventories “verified ramps” or “verified camping.” These were rewritten in this audit branch to distinguish independently named facilities from generic official access/camping planning. A new per-guide official-source pre-trip checklist directs visitors to fishing, access/closure, water-level and regulations sources and warns against treating generic maps as navigation charts.

## Competitive authority benchmark (not yet certified)

For **each lake**, surpassing its controlling agency's *public visitor information* should mean combining accurate primary-source material with original independently useful research, not copying it or inventing data:

1. **Identity & history:** dam purpose, construction vs first impoundment dates, water authorities, original watershed, local history and dated primary sources.
2. **Fishing:** TPWD ratings, seasonal tactics, current survey/stocking and fish-habitat coordinates where officially available, regulations, consumption advisories and invasive species.
3. **Visitor planning:** real ramp/park names, GPS or official entrance map, operator, ramp lane/accessibility/parking/fees, launch conditions and direct status phone/links, beaches/swimming permission, non-fishing recreation, lodging/campground type and official reservation paths.
4. **Maps & imagery:** named facility points sourced to operator maps, accurate visitor directions, differentiating true lake photos from generic photos, rights/creator/date, downloadable original diagrams and source methodology.
5. **SEO + AEO:** one canonical lake guide; meaningful sections rather than repetitive thin pages; county/state-park/park/recreation crosslinks; factual FAQs, updated-at dates only after actual source verification, accessible rendering; compare with controlling-agency site for completeness.
6. **Maintenance:** automatic link and source drift checks, 404/image checks, timed issue audits, current alerts without freezing transient values, production responsive/accessibility tests and fact revalidation by source.

## Thin-intent route control

The lake system exposes eight subject-specific routes for each full lake guide; however, **/reports** and **/guides** routes can otherwise consist only of an empty-state notice. These are potentially repetitive low-value search pages. This audit updated the shared section route to set `robots: noindex, follow` **only when a reports section has no dated reports or a guides section has no verified guide listing**. Substantial pages remain indexable, and visitors can still navigate empty states to see what is not available. This is a pragmatic quality gate, not a claim that all nine URLs per lake should be indexed.

**Follow-up:** the current static sitemap may still list empty `/reports` and `/guides` routes; implement per-lake conditional sitemap emission once the content-presence logic is shared with the sitemap loader. Confirm the final robots/canonical tags with rendered production inspection.

## Verification limitations and acceptance gate

This audit inspected the GitHub repository and sample TPWD primary pages (Lake Fork, Lake Houston, Lake Whitney, Lake Arrowhead, and Proctor Lake). **It did not manually compare all 52 against each agency's full website.** The live texasdefined.com HTML fetches were unavailable to the remote web reader; therefore production status codes, image rendering, accessibility and app interactions are **not independently verified**. Also, TexasDefined may have additional lake references in other fixture, state-park and destination systems outside the two quantified catalogs; 52 + 36 is **not** an assertion that only 88 lake-related URLs exist on the entire site.

The audit is *open* until each page has source-checked, park/ramps/campground visitor facts, distinctive local insight, original/provenanced photos, relevant crosslinks, a usable map, current advisories, and production QA. Website “most authoritative”/search-ranking claims require independent comparisons and search performance evidence; they cannot be guaranteed from a template or CI pass.
