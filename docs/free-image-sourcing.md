# TexasDefined Free Image Sourcing

Use this source list whenever TexasDefined needs a free-to-use photo, historical image, illustration, or map. Prefer an exact match for the subject over generic Texas imagery, and verify the rights statement on the individual asset before publishing.

## Historical-source priority

For Texas history, military history, county history, historic sites, old towns, transportation history and similar topics, search **primary or institutional archives before general stock sites**. A real period photograph, map, poster, postcard, newspaper image or government photograph is usually more useful than a modern reenactment or AI-style historical image.

Preferred order for historical material:

1. The Portal to Texas History
2. Texas Digital Archive
3. Library of Congress
4. Wikimedia Commons
5. PICRYL, preferably with the original institution verified
6. Other institutional or government archives with an item-level reuse statement
7. General stock sources only when authentic archival material does not fit the page

## Search sources

1. **The Portal to Texas History** — Texas historical photographs, maps, newspapers, documents, postcards, architectural material and primary sources from libraries, museums and archives across the state  
   Search: `https://texashistory.unt.edu/search/?q={QUERY}&t=fulltext`  
   Rights rule: check the individual item's **Citations, Rights, Re-Use** information before using it. Do not assume every Portal item is public domain. Preserve the contributing institution and any requested credit line.

2. **Texas Digital Archive** — digitized records, photographs and other historical material from the Texas State Library and Archives Commission  
   Search: `https://tsl.access.preservica.com/`  
   Rights rule: inspect the individual record and any rights or reproduction statement. State ownership or archive custody does not automatically mean unrestricted commercial reuse. Record the collection, creator and archival identifier when available.

3. **Library of Congress Prints & Photographs and digital collections** — high-value historical photographs, maps, posters and government material, including Texas cities, military installations, ranching, transportation and public life  
   Search: `https://www.loc.gov/pictures/search/?q={QUERY}`  
   Rights rule: use the item's **Rights & Access** / rights advisory. Prefer records marked public domain or **No known restrictions on publication** when appropriate, and retain the Library of Congress control number or digital ID in sourcing notes.

4. **Wikimedia Commons** — photos, historical images, architecture, maps and public-domain material, often with archival provenance from the Library of Congress, National Archives, DVIDS, museums and university collections  
   Search: `https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?search={QUERY}&title=Special:MediaSearch&type=image`  
   Rights rule: only use files whose individual file page permits reuse (for example public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA). Preserve creator and license attribution when required. When the Commons file came from an archive, retain the original archive attribution as well.

5. **PICRYL** — public-domain historical media aggregated from libraries, archives and government collections  
   Topic search: `https://picryl.com/topics/{QUERY}`  
   Rights rule: use only items whose asset page explicitly states public domain, CC0 or another reuse status suitable for TexasDefined. Confirm the original source whenever available instead of treating the aggregator alone as authority.

6. **Unsplash** — modern travel and destination photography  
   Search: `https://unsplash.com/s/photos/{QUERY}`  
   Rights rule: use only images specifically available under the Unsplash License. Exclude Unsplash+, Getty, sponsored or other premium/licensed results.

7. **Pixabay** — modern travel, landscape, architecture and general-purpose stock photography  
   Search: `https://pixabay.com/images/search/{QUERY}/`  
   Rights rule: use assets covered by the Pixabay Content License. Exclude sponsored third-party/iStock results. For history pages, do not substitute AI-assisted or generic historical-looking imagery for a real archival source when one is available.

8. **Pexels** — modern travel, city, landscape and general-purpose photography  
   Search: `https://www.pexels.com/search/{QUERY}/`  
   Rights rule: use images available under the Pexels license and follow the asset-level terms. Treat Pexels as a modern-stock source, not as historical evidence.

9. **OpenStreetMap** — maps only, not a general photography source  
   Search: `https://www.openstreetmap.org/search?query={QUERY}`  
   Rights rule: display required OpenStreetMap attribution and follow OpenStreetMap's data/license and tile-usage requirements. Do not bulk scrape or prefetch public OSM tiles.

## Selection rules

- Prefer a photograph of the exact attraction, lake, park, venue, county landmark, town, person, unit, ship, battle, building or subject over generic Texas imagery.
- For historical sections, prioritize true period material or documentary photographs when it materially improves the article.
- For military history, prefer government or archival photographs, battle maps, unit photographs, period base photographs and official records over reenactments.
- For modern travel pages, prefer recent, accurate photography over vintage imagery unless the vintage image is intentionally illustrating history.
- Never publish an image merely because it appeared in Google Images. Follow the result to the original source and verify reuse rights there.
- Never infer public-domain status merely from age, government custody, archive ownership, a "free" search result or the fact that an image appears on a public website.
- Record the original source URL, creator/photographer when available, date, collection or control number when available, license or rights statement, and required attribution at selection time.
- When an archive says **No known restrictions**, preserve that exact rights language rather than upgrading it to "public domain" unless the item record actually establishes public-domain status.
- Do not use a source when the rights statement is missing, contradictory or too ambiguous for commercial publication.
- Existing automated Wikimedia Commons searches must continue to enforce license filtering; this broader list supplements Commons with additional approved search destinations.

## Historical image workflow

1. Search the exact person/place/event in the Portal to Texas History, Texas Digital Archive and Library of Congress.
2. Search Wikimedia Commons for the same subject and inspect whether the file points back to an authoritative archive or government source.
3. Use PICRYL as an additional discovery layer, then verify the original source when possible.
4. Confirm item-level rights before publication.
5. Prefer the highest practical resolution that preserves the archival image accurately.
6. Write useful alt text describing the actual image, not keyword stuffing.
7. Preserve source, date, creator, archive and license/rights data in the image credit.
8. Use modern stock only when the article needs a present-day scene that the historical archives cannot provide.
