Why Cattleman's at Indian Cliffs matters

Cattleman's Steakhouse at Indian Cliffs Ranch grew out of a working ranch setting east of El Paso. The restaurant building was begun in 1972 and opened in 1973, giving the operation more than half a century of history as a West Texas destination built around beef, ranch scenery and the desert landscape.

Its significance comes from the setting as much as the menu. Unlike an urban steakhouse, Cattleman's places the meal inside a broad ranch property near Fabens, making the stop useful for understanding how ranch hospitality and beef culture became part of far West Texas tourism.

What defines the stop

Build this stop into an El Paso-area or Big Bend Country itinerary rather than treating it as a generic steak dinner. The drive, ranch setting and desert surroundings are part of the experience, and the location creates a very different Texas steakhouse context from Fort Worth or the Panhandle.

Menus, event access and operating details can change. Check the official restaurant site before driving from El Paso or farther away, especially when the ranch setting is a central reason for the visit.
