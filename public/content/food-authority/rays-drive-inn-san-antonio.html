Why Ray's Drive Inn matters

Ray's Drive Inn opened on San Antonio's West Side in 1956 under Raymond H. “Ray” Lopez. The restaurant grew from a neighborhood drive-in into a landmark closely associated with the puffy taco, a San Antonio specialty made by frying fresh corn masa so the tortilla inflates before it is filled.

The Lopez family history connects Ray's to a longer neighborhood business tradition. The restaurant's official history traces the family's commercial roots to a grocery operated by Louis and Basilia Lopez and describes how Arturo Lopez later took over Ray's while preserving the original name. The restaurant received a federal trademark for “Puffy Taco” in 1992.

What defines the stop

Ray's matters because the puffy taco is inseparable from San Antonio food culture, and this is one of the places where visitors can connect the dish to a specific family and West Side neighborhood history. The experience is more meaningful in that context than as a generic taco stop.
