Why Smitty's Market matters

Smitty's Market is inseparable from the larger Lockhart barbecue story because it occupies the historic South Commerce Street building where generations of the Schmidt family cooked before Kreuz Market moved to North Colorado Street in 1999. Local Lockhart tourism records the present Smitty's operation as beginning that year under Nina Schmidt Sells, while the building's pits connect directly to the earlier meat-market era.

That continuity makes Smitty's more than another barbecue stop. Visitors can experience an old market layout, open-fire pits and the physical setting that helped make Lockhart a shorthand for Central Texas barbecue. The split between modern Kreuz Market and Smitty's also explains why two of the city's best-known institutions share one family history.

Use Smitty's to understand Lockhart as a barbecue town

A useful Lockhart food trip compares institutions rather than trying to declare one universal winner. Smitty's, Kreuz Market and Black's preserve different branches of the city's barbecue history, and each gives travelers a reason to spend time in the courthouse town instead of treating the stop as a highway detour.

Pair the meal with downtown Lockhart and the broader Caldwell County route. Current hours, payment policies and menu availability can change, so verify operating details before driving.
