Why King's Inn matters

King's Inn grew from a fishing pier and bait stand on Baffin Bay in the mid-1930s. The restaurant's own history describes local cook Blanche Wright serving fish and burgers to fishermen before Cottle and Alta Faye Ware bought the business in 1945 and eventually gave it the King's Inn name.

That origin makes King's Inn a strong Gulf Coast food destination because the restaurant developed directly from the bay, fishing traffic and a family seafood tradition rather than from a generic coastal theme. The Ware family connection and waterside setting have made the stop part of the Riviera and Kingsville-area road-trip identity for generations.

Baffin Bay adds a South Texas seafood story to the statewide map

Texas seafood travel should extend beyond Galveston. King's Inn provides a South Texas counterpoint, connecting fried fish, shrimp and family-style dining with the coastal geography around Baffin Bay and Kleberg County.

Pair the meal with a Coastal Bend or South Texas route and leave enough time for the drive. Service days and menu details can be limited or change seasonally, so confirm current information with the restaurant before departure.
