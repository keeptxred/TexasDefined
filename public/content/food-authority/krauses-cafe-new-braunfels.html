Why Krause's matters

Krause's Cafe connects modern New Braunfels dining with the city's German-Texan heritage. The restaurant's current history traces the Krause family cafe identity to 1959, when Kermit Krause bought Gene's Place from his father and Krause's Cafe became a local gathering place known for meals, beer, dominoes and card games.

The present-day cafe and biergarten consciously carries that heritage forward through German classics, Texas influences and a communal beer-hall format. Its value as a destination comes from seeing German-Texan foodways embedded in New Braunfels rather than encountered as an isolated theme.

What defines the stop

Krause's works best as part of a New Braunfels heritage day alongside the city's German history, downtown and nearby cultural sites. The food story overlaps with sausage, schnitzel, pretzels and the broader German immigration patterns that reshaped Central Texas communities.

Live-music schedules, menus, tap lists and hours are intentionally left to the official site because they change. The durable profile focuses on place, history and regional food identity.
