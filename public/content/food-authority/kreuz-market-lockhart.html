Why Kreuz Market matters

Kreuz Market is one of the institutions that made Lockhart inseparable from the story of Central Texas barbecue. Its history reaches back to Jesse Swearingen's 1875 meat market. Charles Kreuz Sr. purchased that business in 1900, and the Kreuz name became attached to a style built around butcher-shop practicality, prized cuts of meat and smoke rather than elaborate restaurant service.

The business changed hands within and beyond the Kreuz family over the decades, but the continuity of the market tradition is the important part. Kreuz occupied the South Commerce Street building for 99 years. When owner Rick Schmidt moved the operation to North Colorado Street in 1999, pitmaster Roy Perez carried hot coals from the old pits to the new location. The old building became Smitty's Market, making the move itself part of Lockhart barbecue history.

What defines the stop

Kreuz remains closely associated with the Central Texas approach: simply seasoned meat cooked over post oak, sausage rooted in meat-market tradition, and an emphasis on smoke and beef rather than sauce-heavy presentation. That makes the restaurant useful not only as a place to eat but as a living reference point for how the regional barbecue style developed.

For a Texas food road trip, Kreuz also works best in context. Lockhart supports several historically important barbecue businesses within a compact town, so comparing traditions is more informative than treating any single restaurant as an isolated ranking entry.
