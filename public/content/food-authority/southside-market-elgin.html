Why Southside Market matters

Southside Market traces its Elgin roots to 1882, when William Moon raised and slaughtered animals locally and sold fresh meat from a horse-drawn wagon. The company's own history connects smoking and sausage-making to the practical problem of preserving meat before modern refrigeration, a pattern that helps explain the meat-market roots of Central Texas barbecue.

The business later became especially identified with Elgin sausage. That makes Southside a useful destination for understanding how butcher shops, smoked sausage and barbecue developed together rather than as separate food traditions.

Elgin belongs on the Central Texas barbecue-town map

Lockhart receives much of the travel attention, but Elgin adds another important branch to the regional story. A Southside stop can be paired with the town itself, nearby Bastrop and a wider Central Texas road trip instead of being treated as a single restaurant pin.

The current Elgin restaurant and market remain at the business's Highway 290 location. Because service details change, travelers should confirm hours, menu availability and ordering options directly before making a dedicated trip.
