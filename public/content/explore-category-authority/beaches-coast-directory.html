Texas beach, bay shore & barrier-island directory

Reviewed September 6, 2026. This directory separates open-Gulf beaches from bay beaches, coastal parks, barrier islands and informal shoreline access. It also corrects several names that are commonly repeated online as if every place were a conventional public swimming beach. Use the linked managing source for same-day access, surf, closures, beach-driving and facility conditions.

Important classification examples: San José Island is privately owned and public use is limited to the beach below the mean high-tide line; Goose Island State Park is a bayfront park where Texas Parks & Wildlife does not recommend swimming; Yarborough Pass is remote Laguna Madre access rather than a Gulf swimming beach; and Porretto Beach is private property whose public-access status should be checked before a visit.

Goose Island State Park Coast

Bayfront state park focused on camping, fishing, paddling and birding. TPWD specifically does not recommend swimming because the shoreline includes bulkheads, oyster shell, mud flats and marsh grass.

Privately owned undeveloped barrier island across from Port Aransas. Public visitors are limited to the beach below the mean high-tide line; island interior is private and access is by boat.

How TexasDefined classifies the coast

A place can be worth including without being a conventional public swimming beach. Gulf beach, bay beach, coastal park, barrier island, national seashore, shoreline and beach-access labels are kept distinct, and conditional/private-access notes are published where they materially change trip planning.
